# SkeMex: keep useful medical reasoning skills, not every past case

*A Reel interpretation of [arXiv 2606.09365v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09365v4), **Experience Makes Skillful: Enabling Generalizable Medical Agent Reasoning via Self-Evolving Skill Memory**. HarnessRSI Daily score **67**, band ★★. Code: [manglu097/SkeMex](https://github.com/manglu097/SkeMex).*

## What the system changes about itself

SkeMex changes an external **skill repository**, not model weights. It distills interaction trajectories into reusable instructions with applicability conditions and concrete actions. Three branches separate general reasoning strategies, task-level procedures, and fine-grained tool operations. A fixed ReAct executor consults the repository; an agent can adopt, ignore, or override a retrieved skill.

## The loop, and the keep/reject rule

1. Route the new case to a clinical category and retrieve six skills using semantic similarity, estimated utility and decaying memory strength. Retrieval happens once per episode; the chosen context stays fixed during that episode.
2. Solve the case within seven agent steps. A trajectory buffer filters infrastructure errors, repetitive loops, trivial successes and maximum-step failures. It retains informative successes and failures rather than every trace.
3. Every 30 trajectories, process up to 20 retained traces. An analysis pass attributes skill adoption and proposes CREATE, PATCH or NONE; a mutation pass writes the procedural instruction. Novelty and quality gates redirect overlap toward patches and require an actionable trigger and steps.
4. Assess utility from outcome reward relative to a category-specific moving baseline, combined with judged positive, harmful or ignored adoption. The writer can see reference answers or unmet rubric criteria on accumulation cases. This is feedback-supervised memory construction, not learning without an external correctness signal.
5. Govern every two windows: merge similar skills, deprecate utilities below 0.3, and make skills mature at utility at least 0.75 with at least 15 uses. Branch capacities also remove low-utility entries.

The keep rule is therefore **content review plus subsequent utility-based governance**, not a held-out regression test for each proposed edit. Our reading: adoption attribution is an LLM judgment about a trace, not a randomized estimate of a skill's causal contribution.

## Headline result, as reported

Offline evaluation builds memory on training data and **freezes it during testing**. Table 1 averages seven in-domain configurations, mixing accuracy with rubric scores:

| Backbone | ReAct, no external memory | Strongest memory baseline by average | SkeMex |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| DeepSeek-V3.2 | 48.20 | GSEM 52.24 | **56.08** |
| Qwen3.6-Plus | 48.63 | CFM 56.08 | **59.22** |

The paper reports improvements over ReAct of +7.88 and +10.59 points. Qwen consumes the repository built by DeepSeek without re-distilling or rewriting it. These are benchmark averages, **not means accompanied by seed variance or confidence intervals**. The number of runs per cell, the seed and any checkpoint-selection rule for the headline are not specified.

The gains are uneven. DeepSeek's largest in-domain change over ReAct is on MMMU, **46.04 → 66.91**, while MedXpertQA-Text is **33.51 → 35.68** and LiveClin-MM **58.38 → 61.62**. A best-average claim is not a strict win in every column.

## The controls that decide whether the gain is real

- **Offline split and leakage boundary (Appendices D/F).** The stated protocol uses only training traces for skill accumulation; ID tests are held out, and OOD families have zero accumulation cases. Near-duplicates are removed across accumulation and test splits. Thus the offline experience pool excludes evaluation-split traces by design; no quantitative residual-contamination audit is reported. Conversation memory and step history reset per case, but the retained skill repository remains shared across cases. The paper does not fully specify whether all source configurations are combined into one pool, so we do not assume a pooled library.
- **Actual sizes, not reconstructed sizes (Table 10).** ID test counts are LiveClin-Text 101, MedXpertQA-Text 185, HealthBench 175, LiveMedBench 310, LiveClin-MM 185, MedXpertQA-MM 148 and MMMU 139. OOD counts are AgentClinic-Text 214, AgentClinic-MM 120, MediQ 127, MMMU-Pro 56 and MedJourney 264. The category distributions in Table 9 describe the full processed subsets, not the test splits. Preprocessing favors challenging cases and removes modality-invalid or unevaluable records; these are not unfiltered official benchmark scores.
- **Same executor, different memory mechanisms (Section 4.1; Appendix E).** Agentic baselines share the ReAct-style executor, tools, permissions and training split for memory accumulation. Reflexion and CRITIC are adapted to retrieve earlier reflections/critiques. Baselines retain their own memory designs; the paper does not transplant SkeMex's utility/governance mechanism into them. This is a comparison of memory packages, not a mechanism-granted-to-every-baseline control.
- **Selection and tuning boundary (Appendices E–G, pp27–34).** The paper lists default hyperparameters and reports sensitivity studies on HealthBench/LiveMedBench, but does not identify the split that selected those defaults or document an independent validation protocol. Baseline tuning procedures and tuning budgets are also unspecified. Shared execution conditions do not establish equally tuned comparisons. This uncertainty does **not** establish that test-set tuning occurred.
- **Lifecycle ablations (Table 2, online setting).** With tools, prompts and inference budgets fixed within each backbone, DeepSeek's five-configuration average is **53.22** full, **45.34** without writing, **47.16** without utility valuation, **50.02** without governance and **47.64** with similarity-only retrieval. Qwen's corresponding values are **56.19**, **47.13**, **49.32**, **52.50**, **49.93**. These five configurations include AgentClinic-Text and are not the seven-column offline ID average. No variance is supplied.
- **Writing and retention controls (Tables 4–5, 12–13).** DeepSeek falls from **53.22** to **47.56** without buffer gating, **48.82** without draft review and **50.97** with single-prompt encoding. General + Action, the best partial branch combination, reaches **48.65**. Removing maturation yields **47.60**, deprecation **49.27**, merging **49.67**. These support multiple parts of the package, without establishing their effects at matched total optimization cost.
- **Frozen transfer (Tables 14–15).** On OOD families, DeepSeek scores **75.79** versus ReAct **62.01** and the strongest memory average, Evolver **70.35**; Qwen-Plus scores **78.18** versus **64.27** and Evolver **75.21**. The largest ReAct gain is AgentClinic-Text: **34.11 → 68.22** for DeepSeek and **34.58 → 65.89** for Qwen. In a separate fixed-DeepSeek-repository transfer test, Claude Sonnet-4.6 averages **60.27** versus ReAct **49.59**, and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B **58.23** versus **47.71**. The repository is shared; the consuming model changes without target-side rewriting. Baseline transfer rows exist, but equal source-construction budgets are not established.
- **Unresolved OOD reporting disagreement.** For the same DeepSeek offline-OOD setting, p7/Figure 3 and Table 14 disagree about competing baselines. Figure 3 includes Evolver but plots it below GM in the average and MedJourney panels; Table 14 instead makes Evolver the strongest average baseline and the MedJourney winner. We use Table 14 for both comparisons and cannot resolve the disagreement from the PDF (details in SOURCE.md).
- **Online is a different evidence regime (Table 3).** SkeMex's five-benchmark average rises **76.39 → 77.37 → 78.56** across three epochs while memory updates during interaction. Earlier evaluated trajectories can contribute to later memory in this streaming regime; these scores should not be read as frozen held-out generalization. The paper does not clearly state the epoch-level split, reset/reshuffle policy or whether later epochs revisit the same scored cases. We cannot certify an absence of evaluation-case reuse. The other listed online memory baselines also accumulate experience through their own mechanisms.

## Where the gain is absent or small

- **Ties and losses matter.** In offline Table 1, DeepSeek ties Evolver on LiveClin-Text (**92.08**) and Voyager/GSEM on LiveClin-MM (**61.62**). On MedXpertQA-Text, **35.68** versus GSEM's **34.59** is a small margin with no uncertainty estimate.
- **OOD is not uniformly dominant.** DeepSeek's MedJourney **76.52** is below Evolver's **78.79**. AgentClinic-MM is **89.17** versus GSEM **88.33**. Qwen-Plus's MediQ **97.64** is only slightly above several baselines at **96.85**. With no CI, these small differences do not establish statistical superiority.
- **Transfer has exceptions.** Qwen3.6-35B-A3B with transferred skills scores LiveClin-Text **90.04** versus CFM **91.17**, and MedXpertQA-MM **53.78** versus GM **54.03**. In online epoch three, SkeMex's LiveClin-Text **96.04** is below Evolver's **99.01**; LiveClin-MM **67.57** ties Agent KB.
- **More computation is actually used (Tables 16–17).** DeepSeek averages **4.77** interaction steps and **116.06 s** per task versus ReAct **3.17** and **54.48 s**. HealthBench is an exception: **52.36 s** versus **64.19 s**. These are approximate inference wall-clock measurements affected by APIs and services, not a complete accounting of repository construction, donor-model calls, tokens or dollars. Equal maximum budgets do not imply equal realized compute.
- **No repeat-run uncertainty.** The checklist explicitly says statistical-significance tests are not reported. Sensitivity analyses and the training-order study are not multi-seed error bars. The method has not been validated as a substitute for professional clinical judgment.

## Our read

HarnessRSI score **67**, band ★★, evidence level **retained**. The strongest evidence is not the rising online curve: it is a frozen artifact helping held-out cases and unseen benchmark families, including consumption by other backbones. The paper reports extensive lifecycle ablations and partially matched inference conditions, but not run-to-run uncertainty, a complete memory-building cost, an equal-total-compute comparison, or the mechanism granted to competing baselines. The default-selection split, independent validation protocol and baseline tuning procedures/budgets remain unspecified, so selection-independent evaluation and equally tuned comparisons are not established.

Our stored judgment leaves leakage protection unclear. The full PDF does describe near-duplicate removal and train/test exclusion, so that field understates the stated offline safeguards; this is recorded in SOURCE.md without changing the judgment or score. Online evaluation-case reuse remains insufficiently specified. The defensible takeaway is **portable, feedback-governed procedural memory with uneven gains and a real inference-time cost**, not evidence of clinical safety or improvement of the improvement algorithm itself.
