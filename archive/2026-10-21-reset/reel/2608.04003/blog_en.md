# PAST-Bench: does retained experience help a personal agent, and what does Hermes+ add?

*A Reel interpretation of [arXiv 2608.04003v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04003v1) (HarnessRSI Daily score 71, ★★★). Code: [Gen-Verse/PAST-Bench](https://github.com/Gen-Verse/PAST-Bench).*

## What the system changes about itself

PAST-Bench is mainly a benchmark, not a self-modifying optimizer. It tests the layer personal agents already have: a persistent substrate of memory records, skills, profile entries and session history. An agent writes to it in earlier "learn" episodes and must read from it in later episodes, each of which is a fresh session with the context wiped. The suite has 26 scenarios and 204 episodes in four capabilities: memory, procedural reuse, information gathering and update. All tasks are synthetic.

The paper describes no external verifier deciding what is kept (our reading from absence; the E5 step of Hermes+ includes a "Background Review" that the paper does not describe as a filter): the agent chooses what to save. The benchmark instead measures whether keeping helped. Each evaluation episode is run twice with the same prompt, grader, tools and seed, once with access to family-produced state (w/ evolve) and once without (w/o evolve). The gap Δ between the two is the primary metric. The paper says Δ counts as evidence only if it clears the upper bound set by control episodes (no-retention, distractor, stale and wrong-mechanism controls), and it adds a mechanism-evidence score (Mech) from saved artifacts and runtime telemetry. The paper calls the matched design "a strong design control rather than a causal proof".

The paper also builds Hermes+, which adds five mechanisms to the Hermes agent, one per loop stage: E1 plan-time check of saved state, E2 typed memory bindings that render only the current value, E3 ranked, patchable skills, E4 a retrieval gate that withholds a draft answer once and injects a prompt to consult persistence if no read has occurred, and E5 an episode-close step that overwrites old state and flushes it. The five were derived by inspecting low-gain and uneven-gain Hermes traces.

## Headline results, as reported

- **Hermes, seven base models (Table 2, "average across 3 runs").** Every model gains from persistence: Overall Δ from +0.13 (MiniMax-M2.7) to +0.24 (GPT-5.4). Gains are uneven: Memory Δ is +0.23 to +0.37, Update +0.12 to +0.36, Procedural only +0.03 to +0.19 and Information gathering +0.05 to +0.11.
- **Fixed model MiniMax-M2.7, four frameworks plus Hermes+ (Table 3).** Overall Δ / Mech: nanobot +0.13 / 0.57, ZeroClaw +0.12 / 0.55, Agent-Zero −0.08 / 0.39, Hermes +0.13 / 0.64, Hermes+ +0.15 / 0.73. nanobot and Hermes tie on Δ but differ on Mech, which is the paper's example of the same headline gain with different evidence of the intended pathway.
- **Two general-purpose agents (Table 9, MiniMax-M2.7, 3 runs).** Codex CLI Overall Δ +0.10 and Claude Code +0.16, positive on all four capabilities.
- **Hermes+ on MiniMax-M2.7.** Overall Δ +0.13 to +0.15 and Mech 0.64 to 0.73; the clearest gain is Update, Δ +0.12 to +0.24. Persistence-on Overall score is unchanged at 0.66. Our reading: part of the Mech rise may be by construction, because E1 and E4 force reads, E2, E3 and E5 force typed writes, and Mech's recall term credits only episodes that fired the expected retrieval signal (Equation 2); the paper itself says Mech measures consistency with an expected persistence pathway rather than establishing causal necessity.

## The controls that decide whether the gain is real

- **Matched on/off pair (all tables).** The same model, agent, prompt, tools, context window and limits are used on both sides; only access to retained state changes (Appendix E.1). This supports reading Δ as retained-experience value within a model–agent pair. Absolute scores across systems still include each system's own context management.
- **Variance (Table 11).** Only two configurations, Hermes and Hermes+ on MiniMax-M2.7, have three independent runs reported with an SD. Overall Δ is 0.13 ± 0.04 for Hermes and 0.15 ± 0.06 for Hermes+; the authors say the +0.02 difference "is smaller than the run-to-run variation" and do not read it as a stable overall gain. Update Δ moves from +0.12 (SD 0.01) to +0.24 (SD 0.09). No SD is given for any other model, framework or ablation row. Table 2's caption says "average across 3 runs" for all seven models, while Appendix D.5 says only two configurations have three runs; the paper does not reconcile the two. Appendix B.1 says each episode is run across three independent trials, which may be what the caption refers to, but the paper does not say so. No headline seed or run is named; Base and Full rows of Table 4 equal the Table 11 means.
- **Single-mechanism ablations (Table 4, MiniMax-M2.7, rows not cumulative).** Each mechanism helps its target: E2 gives the best Memory persistence-on score (0.80), E3 the best single Procedural Δ (+0.10), E4 the best Info Δ (+0.17), E5 the best single Update Δ (+0.16). The paper does not state the number of runs per row. Each single-mechanism row has a lower Overall persistence-on score than Base Hermes (0.62, 0.60, 0.51, 0.55, 0.55 against 0.66); for example E3 alone drops Memory from 0.77 to 0.46 and Update from 0.62 to 0.35.
- **Interaction (Table 5).** In a "focused Procedural diagnostic" removing E2 raises Δ from +0.085 to +0.108, and removing E3 or E5 lowers it to +0.062 or +0.042. The paper says this is not a full-benchmark estimate. Its Base and Full values (+0.087, +0.085) differ from the Procedural Δ in Tables 4 and 11 (+0.05, −0.02), and the paper does not say how the subset or runs differ.
- **Other base models (Table 6).** Hermes+ was tuned on MiniMax-M2.7. Moved to other models, Overall Δ changes as follows (Hermes to Hermes+): DeepSeek-V4-Pro +0.17 to +0.15, GPT-5.4 +0.24 to +0.24, Claude Sonnet 4.6 +0.20 to +0.22, Claude Opus 4.6 +0.19 to +0.18. The paper counts three of five configurations as matched or improved and calls the transfer "positive but not uniform". Mech goes from 0.64 to 0.73 on MiniMax-M2.7; on the other four it goes 0.71 to 0.69, 0.80 to 0.80, 0.76 to 0.77 and 0.70 to 0.70.
- **Cost (Table 12).** On MiniMax-M2.7, Hermes+ uses 31,859 tokens per episode against 12,615 for Hermes (about 2.5×), with wall-clock 77.4 s against 70.5 s (1.10×); the paper says the extra tokens are mostly added system-prompt context rather than generated output. On the other models the Hermes+ to Hermes token ratio is 0.90‡× (DeepSeek-V4-Pro), 1.5‡× (GPT-5.4), 1.84‡× (Sonnet 4.6) and 0.55‡× (Opus 4.6). ZeroClaw (43,955) and Agent-Zero (53,100) use more tokens per episode than Hermes+ without a higher Δ, and nanobot (10,905) ties Hermes on Δ. The paper has no matched-token baseline row.
- **Judge (Appendix B.4).** Open-ended grading uses MiniMax-M2.7 as the judge, which is also the model Hermes+ was tuned on; the judge was not varied. Against the mean of two blinded humans on 48 samples, the judge is within 0.25 on 68.8% and within 0.5 on 91.7%.

## Where the gain is absent or small

- **Overall Hermes+ gain is inside the noise.** +0.15 against +0.13, with SDs of 0.06 and 0.04. Persistence-on Overall is 0.66 for both.
- **Procedural is worse with Hermes+.** Persistence-on score 0.55 to 0.38 (a drop of 0.17‡), Δ +0.05 to −0.02. The paper describes this as a slight decline in Procedural result.
- **The Update gain is dominated by one family.** Per family (Table 10, 7–8 episodes each), five of seven Update families rose and two fell. Fact correction goes from −0.078 to +0.492 (the largest rise); SOP patch 01 goes from +0.041 to +0.163, rule migration from +0.135 to +0.226, temporary-exception pollution from +0.034 to +0.235 and scoped rule migration from +0.354 to +0.502; recall-then-modify falls from +0.347 to +0.190 and SOP patch 02 goes from +0.017 to −0.127. The doubling of Update Δ is seen on MiniMax-M2.7 (Table 6 against Table 2); on the other four models Update Δ goes (Hermes to Hermes+) from 0.18 to 0.13, 0.34 to 0.31, 0.29 to 0.33 and 0.28 to 0.33.
- **Agent-Zero is negative, and the frameworks ran under different limits.** Agent-Zero's Overall Δ is −0.08 with a 1,200 s per-task budget against 300 s for Hermes and Hermes+, which the paper calls "a methodological footnote rather than a fairness adjustment" while also writing that Agent-Zero "is competitive on score given enough budget". Table 15 also lists 50 iterations for Hermes, 30 for nanobot and a recursion limit of 100 for ZeroClaw; a timed-out or crashed episode scores zero and is not rerun, and the paper reports no timeout counts. The Hermes+ mechanisms were not given to nanobot, ZeroClaw or Agent-Zero: for Agent-Zero and nanobot the paper says they would overlap the framework's own persistence stack, for ZeroClaw it only says they were not added, and it describes the adapters as "not a byte-for-byte default deployment".
- **Task generation.** The tasks, graders and expected artifacts were generated with Codex (GPT-5.4) and Claude Code (Claude Opus 4.6) and checked by three authors (Appendix A.2); GPT-5.4 also has the largest Overall Δ in Table 2 (+0.24). The paper does not test whether the generators favour the models evaluated.
- **Limits the authors state.** Tasks are synthetic and evaluated in isolation; Mech measures consistency with an expected pathway, not causal necessity; Hermes+ is treated "as a diagnostic scaffold rather than a universal improvement".
- **Controls not tabulated.** The paper describes no-retention, distractor, stale and wrong-mechanism controls, and cold episodes "for calibration", but no table reports their scores.
- **Data note.** Two Procedural rows in Table 10 (SOP bootstrap 05 and 06) are identical in three of four cells (Hermes 0.488 / +0.117, Hermes+ persistence-on 0.231) and differ only in Hermes+ Δ (+0.022 vs −0.005); the paper does not comment.

## Our read

HarnessRSI score **71**, band ★★★, evidence level **retained**: later-session gains are attributed to retained state through a matched persistence-on/off ablation. The paper reports that matched ablation, run-to-run SD on two configurations, single-mechanism ablations, a trace-level mechanism score, per-episode cost, and released code. It does not report a held-out split for the Hermes+ design (the failure analysis and the evaluation both use PAST-Bench), numbers for its control episodes, a matched-compute comparison, or the Hermes+ mechanisms applied to the other frameworks. The Hermes+ headline gain is smaller than its own run-to-run variation; the benchmark's finding that gains are uneven across capabilities and models is the better-supported result.
