# EvoSafeHarness: a coding agent searches a per-deployment safety harness — and the controls are single searches

*A Reel interpretation of [arXiv 2609.05903v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05903v2) (Nanxi Li, Yingzi Ma, Yulong Cao, Chong Xiang, G. Edward Suh, Bo Li, Yejin Choi, Dawn Song, Chaowei Xiao; HarnessRSI Daily score 68, ★★). Code: [SaFo-Lab/EvoSafeHarness](https://github.com/SaFo-Lab/EvoSafeHarness).*

## What the system changes about itself

The target LLM agent stays frozen. What is searched is its **safety harness** H = (P, C): P is a bundle of natural-language policies (the target model's system policy plus the policies of auxiliary guard models), and C is an executable runtime program (hooks that run before and after each tool call, keep per-trajectory state, and allow, rewrite or block a call). A separate harness is searched for each model × domain deployment. The search is guided by a domain specification and an archive of earlier candidates, their scores and failure traces; the archive starts from a no-defense harness plus principles distilled from CaMeL and DRIFT.

## The loop, and the keep/reject rule

1. A **Designer** (default Claude Opus 4.8) proposes a new H = (P, C) in free form, from the domain specification and the archive. (Sec. 2 and Appendix D say one outer coding model drives Designer, Analyzer and Criticizer; Appendix A says the Criticizer is Sonnet 4.6 in the warm-start and Criticizer ablation arms. The paper does not reconcile the two.)
2. A **Criticizer** in a fresh context reads the candidate and the specification but not the search tasks, earlier outcomes or the Designer's reasoning. It looks for rules that a rename, relocation or paraphrase would evade; flagged candidates are repaired and re-reviewed.
3. A four-stage **cascade** evaluates survivors: static validation, a smoke test (3 tasks on DecodingTrust-Agent), a confidence gate (12 tasks; a candidate stops only if a one-sided 95% bootstrap upper bound of its score difference from no-defense is below −10 points), then the full 30-task search set. Utility, direct-attack and indirect-attack outcomes are scored separately.
4. An **Analyzer** turns the trajectories into a failure-pattern log and a design-lesson log, which go back into the archive.
5. After a fixed search budget (the number of iterations is not stated in the paper) the highest-scoring candidate (score = utility − ASR, on search data) that completed the full evaluation is returned. Held-out tasks are used only for the final evaluation.

## Headline result, as reported

On DecodingTrust-Agent (five victim models × three domains = 15 searched harnesses; held-out split of 30 benign, 35 direct and 35 indirect tasks per domain), averaged over the 15 cells (Table 1):

| | Utility | Direct ASR | Indirect ASR |
|---|---|---|---|
| No defense | 83.1 | 50.9 | 40.4 |
| CaMeL | 69.7 | 44.3 | 31.1 |
| DRIFT | 77.1 | 49.3 | 35.2 |
| Progent | 56.4 | 14.7 | 6.3 |
| EvoSafeHarness | 79.8 | 12.6 | 7.4 |

Other benchmarks (Table 2): on AgentDojo (three victims) the searched harness reaches 82.8 utility at 0.0 ASR, against 41.0 utility at 0.0 ASR for CaMeL and 71.5 / 27.4 for no defense; the closer baselines are Progent (75.3 utility, 4.8 ASR) and DRIFT (74.6 utility, 1.7 ASR). AgentDojo is run with one attack family (important_instructions); the AgentDyn attack type is not stated beyond "the same general indirect prompt-injection setting". Frozen and applied unchanged to AgentDyn, it gets 75.0 utility at 0.0 ASR (no defense 73.3 / 12.8; CaMeL 0.0 / 0.0). On Agent-SafetyBench (240 held-out tasks, three victims) the mean unsafe-behavior rate falls from 24.6 to 12.3, ASR from 17.2 to 5.8, and utility under attack goes from 61.6 to 63.4. Against a PAIR-style adaptive attacker on one frozen ASB harness (GLM-5.2), cumulative ASR is 10.7 at the start and 17.9 after 16 attempts per task. That study covers one harness, one attacker (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) and one injection surface (ASB's supplemental context).

## The controls that decide whether the gain is real

- **Criticizer ablation.** Rerunning three DTAP searches without the Criticizer gives selected harnesses that score as well or better on the search set but lower on held-out data: held-out score 50.5 vs 69.0 (finance), 76.2 vs 85.2 (telecom), 50.5 vs 81.4 (os-filesystem); the paper reports −19.5 on average. A blind post-hoc code audit (a Sonnet 4.6 reading each selected harness's code and bundle, the same model that acts as Criticizer in these arms) counts 2 token-keyed rules in the reviewed os-filesystem bundle against 4 without review (telecom 0 vs 1, finance 0 vs 0; the paper says the finance gap is not attributable to token keying, and both finance bundles get the same 42.86% ASR on a seven-case rename probe). That probe is the only rename test the paper reports; we found no replay of the final harnesses on renamed or paraphrased held-out tasks. Each arm is one search and one held-out evaluation, with Kimi-K2.5 as the victim; the paper calls these "small controlled studies, not seed-level variance estimates". The Criticizer-off os-filesystem score (50.5) is about the same as the primary-grid search for that cell (51.43, Table A1), so, in our reading, the 31.0-point os-filesystem gap cannot be separated from search-to-search noise.
- **Other ablations (same single-search design).** Starting from the security warm start vs without: held-out score 69.05 vs 59.52, 85.24 vs 73.33, 81.43 vs 71.90 (finance, telecom, os-filesystem). Swapping the outer Designer model (these four arms start from a no-op initialization, i.e. no warm start) changes mean score from 65.71 (GPT-5.6 Terra) to 81.43 (GPT-5.6 Sol) while mean utility stays at 83.33–86.67; the paper concludes the outer model "mainly affects how safe the searched harness is". That 15.7-point spread‡ between single searches is as large as the warm-start (9.52–11.90) and Criticizer (9–31) effects above, so the single-search caveat matters for all of them.
- **Versus another harness optimizer.** An adapted Meta-Harness search on Agent-SafetyBench with GLM-5.2 reaches held-out score 47.3, against 61.9 for EvoSafeHarness. One victim, one search; the paper does not state the Meta-Harness arm's search budget.
- **Cascade.** Replayed offline over 22 frozen candidate banks (93 candidates, 16 of the banks with Kimi-K2.5), the cascade cuts evaluation tokens by 43.6% (206.6M to 116.6M) and selects the same harness as full evaluation in all 22 banks. This is a replay, not an end-to-end rerun of the search, and "Staged" in Tables A3/A4 is the 3+12+30 schedule the banks ran under (Figure 4b and the text call the comparison "full evaluation"): the 43.6% compares the nested cascade with that staged schedule, not with fully evaluating every candidate.
- **Statistics (Appendix I).** Exact McNemar tests on the paired held-out attack tasks: ASR is significantly lower in 13 of 15 cells (the two exceptions are Sonnet 4.6 on finance and telecom, where undefended ASR is already 1.4–2.9). Pooled over cells, 385 attacks that succeed undefended are blocked and 11 new ones succeed. These intervals cover the fixed held-out tasks only, not randomness of the search or of the providers; benign-utility Wilson intervals overlap in all 15 cells.
- **Baselines.** CaMeL, DRIFT, Progent and SafeHarness are ported once (by the authors) and frozen across all victims, domains and suites, "none is tuned from held-out outcomes". They do not get per-deployment search. The only other search-based comparator is the adapted Meta-Harness arm above (one victim, one search, budget not stated); no baseline is reported at matched compute. Progent's average utility (56.4) is 23.4 points below EvoSafeHarness's (79.8).
- **Cost (Appendix H).** The outer optimizer used 9,823 model responses, 40.457M fresh tokens and 2.560B cached-read tokens (no dollar figure recorded). The target-victim runtime ledger records 58,874 calls and $328.74 (the paper says recorded dollars "are not complete billing", so this is a floor), but it mixes search, confirmation, baselines, held-out runs and diagnostics. Per-baseline cost and per-search cost are not given.

## Where the gain is absent or small

- **Progent has lower ASR on two of three domains.** By domain average (Table 1), Progent's direct / indirect ASR is 6.3 / 2.3 on os-filesystem and 4.6 / 6.9 on finance, against 24.6 / 8.0 and 9.7 / 13.7 for EvoSafeHarness, at utility 42.7 and 56.0 against 76.0 and 84.6. On telecom EvoSafeHarness is far lower in ASR (3.4 / 0.6 against 33.1 / 9.7). Progent's overall indirect ASR (6.3) is below EvoSafeHarness's (7.4); the paper calls the two "similar".
- **Weak cells.** DeepSeek-V4-Flash on os-filesystem keeps direct ASR at 74.3 (undefended 94.3). Averaged over the 15 cells EvoSafeHarness cuts direct ASR by 38.3 points (Table A8), but os-filesystem still holds 57 of the 105 remaining successful attacks (finance 41, telecom 7).
- **Unchanged risk types (Table A8).** Client-targeted scams stay at 9/15 (60.0), options solicitation at 3/10 (30.0), telecom finance fraud at 5/30 (16.7) — exactly their undefended rates. The paper says the requested action is in scope and the harm lies in message or answer content.
- **Little headroom or small utility change.** Sonnet 4.6 is almost immune undefended (direct ASR 1.9, indirect 7.6). Average DTAP utility falls from 83.1 to 79.8; with 30 benign tasks per cell (one task is 3.3 points‡) the utility intervals overlap. On AgentDyn, utility is 75.0 vs 73.3 undefended. On ASB, Kimi-K2.6 utility under attack is 65.3 vs 66.7 undefended, and GLM-5.2 ASR ties SafeHarness (8.6).
- **Small search sets and no variance of the search.** The ASB search set is 48 tasks; every DTAP search uses 60 training tasks (30 in the sample the cascade ends on). Kimi-K2.5 on finance, telecom and os-filesystem is searched at least seven times under different configurations (primary grid, four outer models, no warm start, no Criticizer), but the paper does not repeat a search under the same configuration. In Table A1 the primary-grid Kimi-K2.5 searches (earlier Criticizer configuration, Opus 4.8 Designer) score 73.33 / 77.14 / 51.43 on finance / telecom / os-filesystem, while the reviewed arm in Table A3 scores 69.0 / 85.2 / 81.4 for the same victim and domains; the paper notes the configuration difference, and this pair is the nearest thing to a run-to-run spread.
- **Inconsistencies in the paper.** Appendix G says counts are never pooled across direct and indirect attacks, yet "telecom finance fraud 5/30 (16.7)" pools direct 4/15 and indirect 1/15. The held-out split is nominally 30 benign / 35 direct / 35 indirect tasks per domain, but tasks lost to provider outages are excluded (Appendix D) and some Table 1 values (for example 79.3 and 61.8) are not multiples of 1/30 or 1/35; we read this as unequal denominators, so baseline and EvoSafeHarness cells may not be strictly task-for-task paired (our inference; Appendix I describes paired tests on the same frozen tasks).
- **Extra victim.** On Nemotron-3-Ultra (Table A11, same protocol) utility goes from 87.8 to 90.0 and direct / indirect ASR from 70.5 / 48.6 to 2.9 / 8.6. There are no baselines in that table.

## Our read

HarnessRSI score **68**, band ★★, evidence level **retained**: the retained artifact (a searched harness) helps on held-out tasks and, for AgentDojo, on unseen AgentDyn suites. The paper reports held-out evaluation on all benchmarks, zero-shot transfer, a blind code audit counting token-keyed rules (plus one finance rename probe) and Criticizer / warm-start / cascade ablations, per-task statistical tests, and aggregate cost ledgers. It does not report variance across repeated same-configuration searches, a matched-budget baseline (the Meta-Harness arm's budget is unstated), per-baseline cost, or the number of search iterations. The headline table is one search per cell; ablation arms are single searches.
