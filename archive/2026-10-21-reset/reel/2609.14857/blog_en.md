# ModularRSI: five harness modules evolved separately from contrastive trajectories — a reported gain on TerminalBench 2.0, from one evolution run per setting

*A Reel interpretation of [arXiv 2609.14857v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14857v2) (HarnessRSI Daily score 72, ★★★). Code: [IQuestLab/ModularRSI](https://github.com/IQuestLab/ModularRSI).*

## What the system changes about itself

ModularRSI rewrites the **code of a CLI agent's harness** (Terminus-2 in the Harbor framework). The harness is split into five modules — Agent Loop, Observation Management, Tool Use, Context Management and Task Completion Detection — and each module is evolved on its own, from the same starting harness, without sharing intermediate edits. Sandbox set-up, parallel execution and LLM communication stay outside the evolution scope. Model weights are not changed; the retained artifact is harness code. The agent being evolved (DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Preview) is also the Code-Modify Agent that analyses trajectories and writes the patches.

## The signal, and the keep/reject rule

- **Signal.** Each evolution task is rolled out 3 times with a binary reward. Tasks where some rollouts pass and some fail are the *contrastive* group: the agent compares the passing and failing trajectory of the same task. All-fail tasks are paired with an earlier success from a trajectory memory when one exists; all-pass tasks are mined for wasted effort.
- **Choosing what to change.** Similar diagnoses are merged into candidate edits; a candidate's vote count is the number of distinct tasks that support it, and the top K = 10 are implemented, so recurring problems win over one-off failures. Past edits are kept in an evolution history to avoid contradictory changes.
- **Keep/reject.** Every function update must pass three gates in order, otherwise it is rolled back from the recorded diff: a static program check; a diff review in which the same agent looks for task-specific hacks; and an execution check on two tasks sampled from the current batch.
- **Schedule.** Each module is evolved for 3 epochs, then one cross-module integration epoch removes duplicated or conflicting mechanisms (16 epochs in all). The function library is then frozen. At evaluation time an LLM-based composer picks the task-relevant subset of functions.
- **Evolution data.** The authors curated 2,000 executable tasks from external sources (GitHub, Hugging Face, Kaggle, Linux kernel documentation) with a human review and an LLM similarity screen against the benchmarks; two separate evolution sets are drawn from it, 120 TB-related and 120 SWE-related tasks, and each yields its own evolved harness.

## Headline result, as reported

DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Preview, harness evolved on the benchmark-disjoint set, evaluated on TerminalBench 2.0 (89 tasks) and SWE-Bench-Verified (500 tasks), three rollouts per task (Table 1).

| Evaluated on | No evolution | Evolved on the other family | Evolved on the same family |
|---|---|---|---|
| TerminalBench 2.0 accuracy | 47.57 | 49.44 (SWE-related) | 52.43 (TB-related) |
| TerminalBench 2.0 Pass^3 | 30.34 | 30.34 | 35.96 |
| SWE-Bench-Verified accuracy | 73.40 | 75.80 (TB-related) | 76.40 (SWE-related) |

Paired task-level bootstrap intervals for the accuracy change (Table 9): TerminalBench 2.0 +4.86 [3.37, 9.36] and +1.87 [1.49, 5.24]; SWE-Bench-Verified +2.40 [0.73, 3.33] and +3.00 [1.06, 4.26]. The same TB-related harness, applied unchanged to other inference models on TerminalBench 2.0, gives GLM-5.2 59.55 → 61.80 and MiniMax-2.5 41.57 → 44.94 (Table 2). Running the whole procedure with other evolution models gives DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 61.79 → 67.42 and Qwen3.8-Max 61.05 → 64.79 (Table 8, no intervals).

## The controls that decide whether the gain is real

- **Modular vs unrestricted evolution at matched epochs (Table 3).** Joint all-module evolution and non-modular whole-harness evolution were each run for 16 epochs, the same aggregate epochs as ModularRSI, with the same 3 rollouts per task. On accuracy both end below the unevolved harness: 44.19 and 46.44 against 47.57, versus 52.43 for ModularRSI (Table 9: −3.38 [−3.74, −1.50], −1.13 [−2.24, −0.74] and +4.86 [3.37, 9.36]). The comparison is accuracy-specific: both alternatives also fall below the unevolved harness on Pass^3 (24.72 against 30.34), but both are above it on Pass@3 (61.80 and 64.04 against 58.43), and non-modular evolution uses fewer steps (29.03 against 34.70). ModularRSI's Pass@3 is 1.13‡ above non-modular. "Budget" here is epochs; evolution tokens, dollars or wall-clock are not reported.
- **Other optimizers (Table 6).** Meta-Harness and AHE were adapted and reproduced by the authors in Harbor, started from the same Terminus-2 harness and evolved on the same 120 TB-related tasks with the same model for 16 epochs: accuracy 49.44 and 49.81 against 52.43. Their accuracy gains over the unevolved harness are +1.87 [-0.07, 2.24] and +2.24 [-0.03, 2.62]; both intervals include zero, while ModularRSI's own interval, +4.86 [3.37, 9.36], lies above both. The paper runs no direct test of ModularRSI against them. On the 267 rollouts of TerminalBench 2.0 (89 tasks × 3) the gap to AHE is 7‡ successful rollouts (140‡ against 133‡). Tuning budgets and variance for the baselines are not reported. The paper also does not say whether Meta-Harness and AHE were given the function-library management that ModularRSI uses (function merge and the test-time task-aware composer) or its validation gates. This matters: the ablation without library management scores 49.81 in Table 5, numerically equal to AHE (see below). The paper states web search was disabled "for all methods" in this comparison; it does not say whether the Table 1 runs also had it disabled, although ModularRSI's 52.43 is the same number in both tables.
- **Ablations (Tables 4 and 5).** Removing contrastive trajectory analysis gives 50.56, removing function-library management (merge plus the task-aware composer) 49.81, removing the validation gates 48.31 (full 52.43, unevolved 47.57). The library-management ablation therefore accounts for the same 2.62‡ points that separate ModularRSI from AHE. The composer is an LLM call at test time; the paper does not say which model it uses for GLM-5.2 or MiniMax-2.5 or what it costs. Each of the five single-module harnesses already beats the unevolved harness on accuracy (49.44 to 50.56), and integration reaches 52.43. These are single runs without intervals.
- **Transfer.** Evolution on the other task family still helps (above), and the evolved harness helps two other inference models. The cross-model harness was evolved once, with DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Preview on the TB-related set, and only two other inference models were tested.
- **Leakage.** The paper's guard is construction: retrieval guided only by category-level benchmark descriptions, executable validation of each task, manual review, an LLM similarity screen against the benchmarks, and web search disabled in the comparison with other methods. "Benchmark-disjoint" is the authors' claim; the screen is not quantified (no overlap statistic, threshold or count of removed tasks) and there is no separate contamination test.

## Where the gain is absent or small

- **Other optimizers are close.** AHE and Meta-Harness already recover +2.24 and +1.87 accuracy points, with intervals that include zero; the remaining margin to ModularRSI is 2.62‡ and 2.99‡ points on an 89-task benchmark (7‡ rollouts over AHE), with no direct test and an unstated composer/gate confound (above).
- **Pass^3 and steps do not always improve.** The SWE-related harness raises TerminalBench 2.0 accuracy to 49.44 but leaves Pass^3 at 30.34. Average steps per rollout are 35.57 for ModularRSI against 34.70 unevolved (Table 3); individual modules move it both ways (Observation Management 22.50, Tool Use 41.28, Table 4).
- **One evolution run per setting.** The intervals resample tasks only (each task's three runs are averaged and kept together, Appendix G), for a fixed harness; they do not capture variation between evolution runs, and the paper reports no repeated evolutions. The intervals are strongly asymmetric as printed (for example +1.87 [1.49, 5.24] for the SWE-related harness and +1.87 [-0.07, 2.24] for Meta-Harness).
- **The evolution curve.** Figure 6 plots TerminalBench 2.0 accuracy per generation up to generation 22, whereas everything else counts 16 epochs (unexplained). The text calls the improvement "largely monotonic"; the curve is not strictly monotonic (a dip near generation 12) and ends at 52.2‡ against 52.43 in the tables.
- **The SWE-Bench-Verified headline looks like the better of two variants.** Table 10 gives 76.40 for the medium-difficulty SWE-related evolution set and 74.20 for a hard-and-easy set (single runs; Figure 5 covers epochs 0 to 2 only). 76.40 is also the Table 1 headline; the paper does not say whether the headline harness was chosen from these two.
- **Scaling is a single run per point.** One epoch on 600 evolution tasks reaches 57.3‡ on TerminalBench 2.0 (unevolved 47.6‡; only these two points are labelled in Figure 2).
- **Case studies.** The authors state that their case-study runs "contain other updates, so the comparisons do not isolate the effect of a single change".

## Our read

HarnessRSI score **72**, band ★★★, evidence level **retained**: the evolved harness helps on benchmarks that were not used to evolve it, including the other task family and other inference models. The paper reports benchmark-disjoint evolution data, two-way task transfer, cross-model transfer, a comparison with joint and non-modular evolution and with Meta-Harness and AHE at matched epochs, ablations, and task-level bootstrap intervals. It does not report variance across evolution runs, the compute or token cost of evolution (including the test-time composer), interval estimates for the ablations or for ModularRSI against the other optimizers, whether the baselines received the same function-library management and gates, or a quantified contamination check beyond the similarity screen. Our judgment file marks variance and the improver-level comparison as unclear; the full paper's matched-epoch comparisons with other optimizers may support the latter (see SOURCE.md).
