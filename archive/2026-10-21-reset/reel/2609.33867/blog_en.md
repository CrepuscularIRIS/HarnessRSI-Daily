# R² Flow: a skill library that edits itself only when independent verifiers agree — and how much of the gain is the loop

*A Reel interpretation of [arXiv 2609.33867v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33867v2) (HarnessRSI Daily score 69, ★★). Authors: Mingda Zhang, Qiang Huang, Yanjin Li, Zijia Wang, Qika Lin, Xiaoying Tang, Tiesunlong Shen (CUHK-Shenzhen, Fudan, UIUC, Oxford, NUS). The paper is marked "under review" at ICLR 2027. Code: [beita6969/r2flow](https://github.com/beita6969/r2flow).*

## What the system changes about itself

R² Flow has a trainable **Supervisor** that orchestrates a **skill library** for a frozen executor (both Qwen3.5-9B in the main experiments). Two things change: the Supervisor's weights, and the library itself. Training runs in **phases**. Within a phase the library is fixed and the Supervisor is trained with a flow-based objective (R² TB, a sub-trajectory-balance variant) on a **shared-state graph**: histories that differ only in the order of independent steps are merged into one state, so their evidence pools. At the end of a phase the library is edited and the next phase trains on the new library.

## Signal and keep/reject rule

- **Whether to edit:** independent verifier labels on single skill invocations (unit tests, schema validation, assertions, execution status) feed a Beta posterior per skill and context. Wide credible intervals mean "defer"; an upper bound below a quality threshold allows refine or prune.
- **Which skill:** the skill's *flow share* (how much of the trained flow passes through it) and a separate *signed utility* (does it beat an average legal alternative). Prune additionally needs non-positive signed utility.
- **When:** a plateau in the variance of the balance residual ends the phase.
- **Keep rule:** an edit is committed only if it is verifier-eligible **and** paired rollouts on held-out validation queries are non-inferior on success, tempered reward, token cost and latency; otherwise it is rolled back.

## Headline result, as reported

Main results (Table 1): five runs, mean ± SD, one frozen Qwen3.5-9B executor shared by all methods. Twelve datasets: six in-distribution (IID, used to train the Supervisor) and six out-of-distribution (OOD, held out). Each dataset is a fixed 128-record draw under one seed; AIME 2026 uses all 30 problems.

- Averages (Table 1 caption: they pool exact match and F1 over the QA sets and the primary metric over the rest). IID exact-match average, over HotpotQA and TriviaQA only: 93.05 vs 91.02 for SkillOpt, the strongest baseline (+2.03). IID accuracy-type average, over AIME 2026, HealthBench, MBPP+ and ALFWorld: 79.47 vs 73.90 (+5.57). OOD exact-match average, over MuSiQue-Ans and NQ-Open only: 85.78 vs 81.95 (+3.83). OOD accuracy-type average, over MATH-Hard, GPQA, SWE-bench and WebShop: 78.63 vs 75.78 (+2.85, over Trace2Skill).
- Strongest baseline varies by column. Examples (R² Flow vs strongest baseline): AIME 2026 76.00 vs 67.33 (SkillOpt); ALFWorld 88.75 vs 85.00 (Trace2Skill); SWE-bench 44.22 vs 42.97 (SkillOpt); GPQA 84.84 vs 83.91 (FlowSteer); MATH-Hard 95.94 vs 94.84 (Trace2Skill).
- The "Δ" column in Table 1 (for example +33.1 on HotpotQA EM, +63.0 on NQ-Open EM) is the gain over the **Qwen3.5 backbone answering directly**, a column near the bottom (SFT is lower on AIME 2026, HealthBench, MBPP+, ALFWorld and WebShop; SFT and GRPO† fall below it on the IID accuracy-type average); the gain over the strongest baseline is much smaller. The R² Flow row matches the full-model row of Table 2, whose OOD mean (81.02) is the paper's phase-8 value, so the headline is the end of the run, not a best-of-phases pick (our reading of the two tables).

## The controls that decide whether the gain is real

- **Component ablations (Table 2, five-run means, no SD reported).** All 108 ablated cells (nine variants × twelve datasets) fall below the full model, by 0.31 to 14.00 points. Largest effects: returning to a history tree (−7.31 IID, −5.99 OOD); reward-derived instead of verifier labels (−3.34 IID, −5.00 OOD); resetting the phase state (about −5 on both splits); a uniform backward policy (−4.52 IID); ranking by share alone (−3.61 IID). Smallest averages: fixed phase length on IID (−1.58) and reading the shares off the learned flow on OOD (−1.61; −2.63 IID).
- **How much is architecture, how much training.** With an *untrained* Supervisor, the R² Flow architecture already adds 13.71 IID points over the frozen backbone; training adds 18.07 more (Table 2).
- **Same-framework objective comparison (Figure 5a,b).** GRPO, DB, TB, TTB, SubTB and R² TB were compared as the Supervisor objective. R² TB leads every IID dataset and beats the others on OOD by 1.69 to 6.17 points, for 7.5% more GPU time per step than GRPO (2,587 vs 2,406 GPU-seconds; 832 s vs 788 s wall-clock). The closest, SubTB, is within 1.09 points on three datasets but 6.67 points behind on AIME 2026 (30 problems).
- **Do verified edits keep paying off? (Figure 5c–e).** The mean OOD score rises from 71.2 to 81.02 over eight phases and dips once. 33 of 37 committed edits raise the held-out verified score (precision 0.89). With reward-derived labels, 25 of 44 edits raise the training reward but lower the verified score; accuracy peaks at phase 3 and declines. A frozen library stays between 73.4 and 74.8 after the first phase; fixed-length phases end at 78.99.
- **Injected harmful skills (Figure 5e).** Of twelve injected harmful skills, R² Flow prunes eleven; share-only ranking keeps eight, Bayesian-Agent six, SkillFlow seven. The paper's text does not describe how the injected skills were constructed, and these two comparators are not Table 1 baselines.
- **Executor transfer (Figure 4a,d).** With the trained Supervisor fixed (it was trained with a Qwen3.5-9B executor), six other frozen executors (glm-5.3, v4-flash, haiku-4-5, grok-4.5, gpt-5.6-luna, gemini-3.8-flash) gain 15.70 to 24.64 points on average; weaker executors gain more (Pearson r = −0.97). This compares each executor answering directly with the same executor inside R² Flow, so it mixes the Supervisor's orchestration and the skill library; no other orchestration method was run on these executors.
- **Variance.** Table 1 gives SDs over five runs and the paper reports Welch tests (p < 10⁻³ for the average gains over each baseline). The paper does not say what varies between runs (training, library evolution, or only sampling). Several baseline cells have SD exactly 0.00 (Trace2Skill on AIME 2026, SkillOpt and AFlow on MATH-Hard) and most SDs are around one point or below, so the run-to-run variation is small; the tests rest on those SDs, not on sampling error of the 128-record test sets. One record is worth about 0.78‡ points and one AIME problem 3.33‡ points; the binomial standard error of a single accuracy near 85% on 128 records is about 3.2‡ points (our unpaired approximation), larger than the roughly one-point margins noted below.
- **Edit precision.** The abstract claims better "library-edit precision" than skill-evolution baselines. The precision numbers shown are 0.89 for R² Flow against 0.43 for the "− Verifier" ablation (Figure 5d); no precision is reported for Trace2Skill or SkillOpt. The 0.89 is a rise in the "held-out verified score"; the paper's text does not say whether it uses the same queries and verifiers as the paired validation gate that admits edits.

## Where the gain is absent, small or unverified

- **Averages mix few datasets.** The exact-match averages cover two QA datasets each; the accuracy-type averages cover four. The paper's "leads every IID dataset and metric" is true cell by cell in Table 1, but several cells are narrow.
- **Narrow margins.** The paper itself calls the OOD margins on GPQA and MATH-Hard "about one point": 84.84 vs 83.91 and 95.94 vs 94.84 (0.93‡ and 1.10‡), against R² Flow SDs of 1.18 and 0.86. SWE-bench is 44.22 vs 42.97 (1.25‡; SD 1.18). Individual narrow cells are of the size of one run-to-run SD.
- **AIME 2026 carries a large share of the accuracy-type gain.** AIME has 30 problems. Its gap over SkillOpt, 76.00 vs 67.33 (8.67‡), is 39%‡ of the 22.28‡ summed points behind the +5.57 IID accuracy-type gain over SkillOpt; HealthBench (61.89 vs 54.53, 7.36‡) is 33%‡. AIME does not enter the exact-match average.
- **Part of the gain over the backbone appears before training.** With an untrained Supervisor the R² Flow architecture already adds 13.71 of the +31.78‡ IID points over the frozen backbone, but that row still sits far below the skill-evolution baselines on QA (HotpotQA EM 77.03 vs 88.12 to 89.53; TriviaQA EM 77.81 vs 89.53 to 92.50). The paper does not say what the "untrained" row contains beyond the Supervisor being untrained.
- **IID evaluation vs training data.** The six IID datasets "train the Supervisor". The paper states that the validation set is disjoint from the training queries and from every evaluation record, and that the OOD sets are used only for final evaluation. It does not state that the IID evaluation records are disjoint from the IID training queries.
- **Baseline treatment is not fully specified.** All methods share the executor, tools, retrieval corpus and inference budget (the DeepSeek-V4-Flash column is a different model answering directly). The paper does not state tuning or evolution budgets for AFlow, FlowSteer, Trace2Skill or SkillOpt, whether they received verifiers, or whether the direct-answer rows have the tools and retrieval corpus. FlowSteer (a Table 1 baseline) and SkillFlow (a Figure 5e comparator, also the source of the TTB objective) are cited to Zhang et al. 2026d and 2026e, whose author lists begin with Mingda Zhang, this paper's first author, and include Tiesunlong Shen and Xiaoying Tang.
- **Cost.** The 7.5% (GPU) and 5.6% (wall-clock) extra time over GRPO are whole-step figures. On the learner side R² TB uses 433 vs 252 GPU-seconds per step (+72%‡) and 3,121,184 vs 2,510,218 tokens (+24%‡). We found no cost for verifier calls, paired validation or library edits, and none for the skill-evolution baselines. The paper has no limitations section.

## Our read

HarnessRSI score **69**, band ★★ (one point below ★★★), evidence level **retained**: the retained artifact (the evolved skill library plus trained Supervisor) is shown to help on six held-out datasets, not used to select it. **Reports:** held-out OOD datasets, executor transfer, ablations including a retained-phase-state ablation, five-run mean ± SD for the main table, per-step training cost, a stated shared inference budget, a harmful-skill pruning test, code. **Does not report:** tuning or evolution budgets of the baselines, cost of verification and edit steps, SDs for ablations, edit precision for the skill-evolution baselines, a contamination audit of the benchmarks (the paper states only that the validation set is disjoint from training queries and evaluation records), a limitations discussion.
