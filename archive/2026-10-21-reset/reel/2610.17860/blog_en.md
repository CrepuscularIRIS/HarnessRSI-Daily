# Socratic Skill Learning: a coach reviews while a model writes its own Skill — clear gain over no Skill, smaller and noisier gains over the other protocols

*A Reel interpretation of [arXiv 2610.17860v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17860v1) by Letian Zhu, Han Li, Yifan Yao, Xinping Lei, Xinyu Che, Jiahao Wang, Dianyi Wang, Yucheng Fu, Jiakai Wang, Zhiqi Bai, Wenbo Su, Jiaheng Liu (Nanjing University; Alibaba Group). HarnessRSI Daily score 69, ★★. Code: [NJU-LINK/Socratic-Skill-Learning](https://github.com/NJU-LINK/Socratic-Skill-Learning).*

## What the system changes about itself

The artifact that changes is an **Agent Skill package**: a `SKILL.md` plus optional scripts, references and assets. Model weights are never updated. In Socratic Skill Learning (SSL) two models work on one task family. The **Student** (the same model that will later solve the tasks) is the only agent that operates the environment and the only author of the package. The **Educator** (a stronger model) has read-only access to the Student's recent work and the live package, and can explain, challenge, diagnose, propose a check, or stay silent. The Student may question, test, adapt or reject the advice.

Acquisition uses one executable **anchor** task and a public family specification (invariants and permitted variation). Each of 3 rounds ends with a fresh solver testing the package on the reset anchor; the trajectory and verifier feedback go into the next round. Only the **final package** is frozen and evaluated, in fresh solver sessions, on held-out members of the same family. The paper describes no best-round selection or accept/reject gate on package edits.

Two schedules decide when the Educator reviews:

- **Fixed-Window SSL**: a review after every g completed Student tool calls (g = 10 in the main condition; 5 and 20 are compared), plus a final review per round.
- **Issue-Guided SSL**: the Educator's lessons become persistent *learning issues* (focus + acceptance criterion + linked revisions). After an initial review (at the first revision or after a warm-up window of tool calls), a review happens only when an issue opens, changes or reopens; a verified closure needs a successful post-lesson check, and contradictory fresh-solver feedback can reopen an issue.

## The benchmark and the headline

The authors built **SKILLTRANSFERBENCH**: 35 task families, 408 executable cases, six domains, adapted from six existing benchmarks by experts working with agents. The main cohort keeps families with at least 5 verified held-out cases and at least two non-perfect outcomes in catalog-wide No-Skill screening: 22 families, 155 held-out cases. The paper does not say whether the screening runs are independent of the No-Skill repeats reported as the baseline; if they overlap, selecting on non-perfect No-Skill outcomes could inflate every method's gain (our inference, not a paper finding). Scores are family-macro Avg@3 (equal weight per family; 3 independent acquisition runs per family and condition). Student and solver are Qwen3.7 Plus; the Educator or direct author is Qwen3.7 Max.

The two SSL schedules rank first and second overall and in each of the six domains. Fixed-Window SSL leads adapted Trace2Skill by 3.99 points (95% paired family-bootstrap CI [1.45, 6.49]) and adapted SKILL-KD by 4.99 points ([1.79, 8.48]). Full-pass rate (every private check passes) rises from 32.50% with no Skill to 49.49% (Fixed-Window) and 50.82% (Issue-Guided). The headline utility is the mean of three repeats (Fixed-Window 0.772, 0.799, 0.831; SD 0.030), and every SSL repeat exceeds the matching No-Skill repeat (0.719, 0.730, 0.721); so do the three adapted SKILL-KD repeats (0.746, 0.756, 0.750). Repeats for Trace2Skill, Expert Authoring and Post-hoc Teaching are not reported.

## The controls that decide whether the gain is real

- **Same solver, same held-out cases and verifier for all methods.** Expert Authoring, Post-hoc Teaching and both SSL schedules use 3 acquisition rounds with fresh-solver anchor feedback; in Expert Authoring the external Max model, not the Student, explores and writes the package. The adapted Trace2Skill (parallel patching over trajectories from a seed package) and SKILL-KD (three anchor visits with rule edits) do not use that round loop. Having Max write the package itself (0.728) is no better than no Skill; the CI of its gain spans zero. Fixed-Window SSL beats it by 7.29 points ([3.62, 11.44]) and Post-hoc Teaching by 5.31 ([2.34, 9.09]), with 21% fewer acquisition tokens than Post-hoc Teaching.
- **Three model pairs (shared lite subset: 10 families, 77 cases).** Gain over No Skill, in points, for Fixed-Window / Issue-Guided / Expert Authoring / Post-hoc Teaching: Qwen +4.1 / +4.5 / +1.1 / −0.7; DeepSeek +3.1 / +2.5 / +1.2 / +0.5; Kimi +8.2 / +7.4 / +5.8 / +4.1. DeepSeek and Kimi use a single repeat. The Fixed-Window-minus-No-Skill intervals are [1.02, 8.03] (Qwen), [−4.63, 13.03] (DeepSeek) and [2.48, 15.62] (Kimi), so the DeepSeek gain of +3.1 is not distinguishable from zero. Fixed-Window SSL beats Post-hoc Teaching on all three pairs (by 4.88, 2.54 and 4.09 points), but no interval is given for that contrast on this subset, and the paired CI of Fixed-Window minus Expert Authoring includes zero in all three pairs ([−0.83, 7.14], [−1.93, 7.15], [−2.33, 7.98]).
- **Review schedule (Qwen, main cohort).** Fixed g = 5: utility 0.776 at 9.56 M tokens per run; g = 10: 0.801 at 6.89 M; g = 20: 0.776 at 6.61 M; Issue-Guided: 0.805 at 5.72 M. Issue-Guided SSL uses 60.6 M Educator tokens against 158.2 M for g = 10 (61.7% fewer), 16.9% fewer total acquisition tokens, and 2.75 times the transfer gain per Educator token. On DeepSeek and Kimi it uses 63.8% and 77.4% fewer Educator tokens; total acquisition tokens fall for Kimi but rise for DeepSeek (20.01 M to 21.11 M per run).
- **Process evidence (observational).** The fraction of regular reviews that deliver a lesson falls from 73.0% in round one to 16.1% in round three while anchor utility rises from 0.824 to 0.990. After a lesson the next Student call is 23.9 points more likely to write or edit a file ([13.0, 36.1]) and the package is 38.6 points more likely to change within ten calls ([10.7, 57.8]); that second contrast covers 112 of 193 ten-call windows, the others lacking a comparable Skill pair at both ends. The authors state these are associations, since review decisions respond to the Student's ongoing work. A single case (HTML finance verification) shows the Student replacing generic arithmetic with financial relations and a fresh solver passing 4/4 checks on an unseen report. It is one repeat of one family, and that family does not favour SSL: Expert Authoring scores 1.000 against 0.917 for Fixed-Window SSL (Table 9).

## Where the gain is absent, small or within noise

- **None of the four baselines clears zero.** The CIs of the gain over No Skill for Expert Authoring, Post-hoc Teaching, Trace2Skill and SKILL-KD all include zero; only the two SSL schedules have CIs above zero.
- **Fixed-Window and Issue-Guided are not separated.** 0.801 against 0.805 on the main cohort, with repeat-level SD 0.030 (Fixed-Window) and 0.036 (Issue-Guided). On the shared lite subset the Issue-minus-Fixed CIs include zero for all three pairs ([−0.84, 1.44], [−5.34, 3.14], [−4.72, 2.89]), and DeepSeek and Kimi point slightly the other way (0.784 vs 0.779; 0.762 vs 0.753). The paper itself words it as retaining utility within one percentage point of Fixed-Window SSL; that is similar utility with fewer Educator tokens, not a utility gain. No main-cohort interval for Issue-minus-Fixed is given, so "not separated" on the main cohort is our inference from the repeat SDs.
- **The review interval is not a tuned knob we can read off.** Utility is non-monotonic in g (0.776, 0.801, 0.776 for g = 5, 10, 20); the 0.025‡ gaps are of the order of the Fixed-Window repeat SD of 0.030, and the paper does not say how g = 10 became the main condition.
- **Gain is uneven across families.** Fixed-Window SSL improves on No Skill in 16 of 22 families. Four families fall: Bank transaction filter 0.678 to 0.593, SPARQL professors 0.706 to 0.680, Financial documents 0.921 to 0.913, XLSX structure 0.933 to 0.923; ACL inheritance and SQLite truncation are unchanged. The four largest gains (DB WAL recovery 0.473 to 0.884, Flask 0.408 to 0.690, Organization JSON 0.778 to 1.000, HTML finance verification 0.731 to 0.917) account for about 5.0‡ of the 7.75 points of macro gain, and they are not SSL-specific: on DB WAL recovery adapted Trace2Skill (0.905) and SKILL-KD (0.901) beat Fixed-Window SSL (0.884); on Organization JSON Expert Authoring, Post-hoc Teaching and SKILL-KD also reach 1.000; on HTML finance verification Expert Authoring (1.000) and Post-hoc Teaching (0.944) are above Fixed-Window SSL (0.917). Case by case (155 cases), Fixed-Window SSL improves 72, leaves 59 unchanged and regresses 24.
- **Baseline order differs by cohort.** On the main cohort Post-hoc Teaching (0.748) is above Expert Authoring (0.728); on the shared families it trails Expert Authoring in every pair. The order flip is within noise: the paired intervals for the Post-hoc-minus-Expert contrast include zero on the main cohort ([−1.57, 5.64]), and the Expert-minus-Post-hoc intervals on the shared families ([−1.28, 6.05], [−0.89, 2.23], [−1.13, 4.76]) do too. The paper reads the shared-family ordering as favouring guided practice over post-hoc review.
- **Cost.** The Educator tokens saved by Issue-Guided SSL are counted in tokens (cached input included), not dollars. Per-run tokens: Expert Authoring 4.95 M, Post-hoc 8.71 M, Fixed-Window 6.89 M, Issue-Guided 5.72 M. Adapted Trace2Skill and SKILL-KD costs are not on the same scope (the SKILL-KD audit gives 1.94 M tokens per run).
- **Scope the authors state.** The study is a shared single-anchor setting, and the Limitations section says further controlled comparisons could isolate how much the Educator, review timing and issue structure each contribute. Issue-Guided SSL changes the review trigger, the context and the follow-up policy together.

## Our read

HarnessRSI score **69**, band ★★, evidence level **retained**: the frozen package helps on held-out family members evaluated in fresh solver sessions, but the improvement mechanism itself is compared only as protocol variants (who writes, when review happens). The paper reports held-out evaluation on unseen variants of each family, paired family-bootstrap intervals with three repeats on the main cohort, three model pairs on a 10-family subset, token costs for four protocols and review schedules, and a process analysis. It does not report a matched-token comparison across the protocols, a contamination or memorisation check against the public source benchmarks (it states that private tests and reference solutions are kept outside agent workspaces), transfer to a different family, same-scope costs or tuning budgets for the adapted Trace2Skill and SKILL-KD, or an ablation isolating the Educator, review timing and issue structure. The benchmark was built by the authors and the main cohort was chosen by No-Skill screening.
