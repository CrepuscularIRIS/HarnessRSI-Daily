# SEAR: does an inherited skill library really help a robot agent? It depends on the model — and sometimes it hurts

*A Reel interpretation of [arXiv 2610.18792v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18792v1) (HarnessRSI Daily score 79, ★★★). Code, as listed in the paper: [Self-Evolving-Agentic-Robotics/SEAR](https://github.com/Self-Evolving-Agentic-Robotics/SEAR).*

## What the system changes about itself

SEAR is a framework and benchmark rather than one new learning method. A language-model agent improves a robot in one of three ways: it writes a program that becomes the policy (code-as-policy), it stays in the control loop and acts live (agent-as-policy), or it acts as a researcher that fine-tunes a vision-language-action model (auto-research). Between tasks, the one thing that carries over is a **library**: versioned code skills and written memories harvested from the best policy of each finished task. Fine-tuned checkpoints and their data do not carry over. The model weights of the language model never change.

## Signal and keep rule

During a task the agent works in its own container with a budget (one hour per simulated task, two on RoboLab; up to 12 hours per physical task). A separate sealed judge holds the initial states: validation seeds are graded and reported back during development, test seeds are graded once at the end, and only the frozen policy with its imported skills is judged. On hardware an external evaluator judges before-and-after images, and practice stops after ten consecutive successes. What persists is whatever the agent harvested; the loop has no step that re-checks a saved lesson. SEAR's contribution is the set of controls placed around this loop.

## The controls

- **Library or no library:** Vanilla starts every task with an empty library; Lineage inherits and extends it.
- **Trying more often:** best-of-3 splits a *Vanilla* session's budget into three fresh attempts and keeps the best on validation (it is never given a library). The paper says it should not be read as equal compute: restarts have separate submission allowances and can use different numbers of tokens and environment steps. On the LIBERO-family tasks the short attempts are told their 20-minute limit, which makes them submit earlier and more often; the long sessions are not told their budget.
- **Luck:** three independent runs per model and condition — except auto-research on LIBERO, LIBERO-PRO and RoboLab, which is one run, and two cells of Table 6 that have two runs.
- **Familiar tasks:** five generalization tiers, from new initial states of the practiced task (T1) to the physical robot (T5).
- **Privileged information:** a sealed judge, and an audit of the shortcuts agents found (see below: the seal is "not absolute").

The study covers seven frontier models — five in simulation, three on hardware, with Opus in both — over 330 simulated tasks in five families and two physics engines, five physical tasks, and over four years of agent runtime. The library, best-of-3 and crossover comparisons use the five simulation models only; the hardware runs have no library and no best-of-3, and are one practice run per agent. Lineage and Vanilla are matched on current-task budget, but the experience that built a lineage's library remains part of its history, which Vanilla does not have.

## Results as reported

- **The library helps most in the middle (a trend).** Over 25 model–family pairs, the mean gain is +0.9 points where the model already scores above 80% without it (8 pairs), +12.0 points between 30 and 80% (15 pairs; the mean includes the pair where the library hurts) and +3.0 points below 30% (2 pairs, both RoboLab, one of them DeepSeek-v4-flash on only the first 58 tasks). The paper fits a curve through the 25 pairs.
- **It can transfer between models — in one test.** On RoboLab, GPT-5.6-sol scores 46.9% with no library and 42.4% with its own, but 60.6% with the library Opus wrote and 55.7% with Kimi-k3's. This rests on one recipient model on one family, with one selected donor lineage per model (three runs over 105 tasks each); only the inherited library differs. Reuse across simulators is shown by skill imports (Opus's lineages imported 202 distinct skills 660 times on the first half of RoboLab), not by an isolating control.
- **A modest edge over a rerun's noise.** Over 1,510 model–task pairs, the paper compares each Lineage repetition with a third Vanilla repetition. These are shares of comparisons, not success-rate points: on pairs with some Vanilla success, a Lineage repetition is above the reference in 33.7% of cases against 26.1% (margin 7.6), and below it in 23.1% against 31.9%; on 276 unsolved pairs, 32.5% against 22.5% (margin 10.0). On LIBERO a third Vanilla repetition does as well.
- **One long session is not always better than three short ones.** It wins when a short attempt would end before the model's first success: on LIBERO-RIG, Opus gets 96.7% from one long session against 30.6% for best-of-3. Part of such gaps is failure to submit — Kimi-k3 submits a policy in 10% of its 40-minute attempts against 68% of its two-hour runs — and the paper warns that selecting by validation overstates the winner (GPT-5.6-sol's RoboLab winners lose 6.6 points from validation to test). GPT-5.6-sol reaches its first success at a median of 21 minutes on RoboLab and gets 46.9 against 48.5.
- **Success falls with distance from practice — not one controlled ladder.** For Opus the code-policy tiers read 87.8, 59.7, 33.3 and 28.7% (T1 to T4); the matching VLA scores are 73.2, 55.3, 43.1 and 10.2%, each from a single run, and the VLA beats code on T3. The tiers are averaged over different practice–test family pairs, T1 and T2 use frozen policies while T3 and T4 follow self-exploration, and T5 (35.6%) is not a transfer result: it is Opus on new initial states of its own hardware tasks, starting from an empty library, with no VLA run.

## Where the gain is absent or negative

- **Strong models gain almost nothing.** The mean gain is +0.9 points, with changes of both signs; on LIBERO the library moves Opus (94.4 against 95.2%) and GPT-5.6-sol (88.3 against 87.1%) by less than a run's standard deviation.
- **Some large gains are noisy or partial.** Gemini-3.6-flash's gain of 46.6 points on LIBERO-RIG comes from two runs with a standard deviation of 18.9; DeepSeek-v4-flash's RoboLab-with-library score covers only the first 58 tasks, and its LIBERO-RIG column is its successor model, DeepSeek-v4.1-flash.
- **It can hurt.** GPT-5.6-sol on RoboLab drops from 46.9 to 42.4% with its own library; the paper attributes this to what the library holds, mostly location recipes and few grasp checks.
- **Mistakes persist.** In self-exploration (no judge), one Opus run saved a faulty success verifier as a fact; it was wrong on 91% of 699 later rollouts, and the run scored 4.6% against 15.5 and 23.6% for its sibling runs. This is one run of one model.
- **Practice overstates success on a real robot.** Six practice runs reached ten successes in a row; only one then succeeded on all ten staged scenes, and two succeeded on only four. Hardware results are one practice run per agent (as frozen code policies, Fable 5.1 70.2%, GPT-6-astra 41.4%, Opus 35.6%); GPT-6-astra scores 88.0% acting live against 41.4% as code, on five staged scenes per task.
- **The seal is "not absolute".** Opus parsed the object XML in 115 of 179 LIBERO runs and the scene USD in 1,533 of 1,743 RoboLab runs — allowed, and it "helped", but a physical robot cannot do it. 47% of GPT-5.6-sol's graded RoboLab policies stop or branch on `env.check_success()`, against 5% of Opus's. When the authors audited the libraries meant to seed task-blind lineages, the post-LIBERO Opus library already quoted a hidden instruction word for word, and the post-LIBERO-PRO library matched 27 of 160 LIBERO-PRO instructions, so it could not be used.
- **Limits stated by the authors.** Three runs per condition bound what can be told apart, and replicate spread often exceeds the gap between neighboring models. Budgets are matched in wall-clock time, not compute, and the physical runs start from an empty library, so sim-to-real transfer is not measured.

## Our read

HarnessRSI score **79**, band ★★★, evidence level **retained**: a retained artifact (the library) is shown to help on data not used to select it, but the improvement mechanism itself — how skills are harvested and kept — is not varied or compared against alternatives, and the cross-model and cross-simulator evidence has no further isolating control. The paper reports held-out TEST scores, cross-task transfer, three-run variance (one run for auto-research on three families), a best-of-3 restart control that is not compute-matched, a no-library control, a single-recipient cross-model test, and a leakage audit that itself found open shortcuts. Its budget matching is wall-clock only, and its hardware results are single runs.
