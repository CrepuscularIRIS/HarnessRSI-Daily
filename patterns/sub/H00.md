# H00 — Reflect on Outcomes into Transferable Lessons
_parent: **Abstract Experience into Reusable Guidance** (`abstract_experience_into_guidance`) · corpus support: 57 papers (accepted 10 · rejected 13 · unlabeled 34)_

## Tactical pattern
After each attempt the agent looks back at what happened — ideally by contrasting a success against a failure on
the same or a similar task, or winners against losers inside one sampled group — and writes an abstracted lesson,
principle, strategy, or template, often with an explicit trigger condition, into a persistent store that is
retrieved into later contexts. The model stays frozen; the accumulated lesson set is the learned state. The
decisive design choice is the grounding signal and the abstraction level: a lesson must be tied to an observed
outcome (ground truth, execution, a verifier, or at worst self-judgment) and lifted just far enough above the
episode that it fires on a new task without becoming generic advice. Success is certified by gains on tasks that
did not produce the lessons, plus an ablation showing the stored lessons — not the extra retries or the extra
exploration compute — carry the gain.

## Step-by-Step
1. Fix the grounding signal before designing the reflector. Decide what tells a good attempt from a bad one
   (labels, execution, a verifier, a self-judge) and state its error rate; members whose lessons rest on an
   unreliable evaluator report reflections that stall in local minima or store flawed rules.
2. Extract lessons by contrast, not by narration. Pair successes with failures (or rewarded with unrewarded
   rollouts in a group) so the lesson isolates what actually differed; note when a group has no loser or no
   winner and the contrast degenerates.
3. Choose the abstraction level deliberately — a parameterized concept, a trigger-conditioned principle, or a
   reusable template — and test that it transfers across task instances, not only to retries of the same
   instance. Lessons that only help a second attempt on the same item are retry tricks, not transferable guidance.
4. Separate the lesson-producing stream from the evaluation stream. Freeze the store before testing, or report
   results with and without online updates on test items; letting memories keep updating during evaluation, or
   reusing requests already seen while building the library, is the leakage gap that recurs across this cluster.
5. Budget-match and ablate. Compare against the same frozen model given the same number of calls, retries, and
   exploration rollouts, and remove the store while keeping the loop; comparisons across different model sizes
   and training budgets, with no variance estimate, are what sink otherwise plausible members.

## Differentiation within parent
H00 grows its store mainly by appending reflections distilled from the agent's own trajectories. H01 is defined
by the editing policy over entries already in the store (update, merge, delete, forget), not by how a new lesson
is extracted. H02 compiles portable, structured, versioned skill packages from many sources (pooled sessions,
guides, external resources) rather than free-form lessons from one agent's outcomes.

## When to pick this one
The tell: the same agent faces a recurring task family with a trustworthy per-attempt outcome signal, and
failures share causes that can be named in a sentence. Prefer H01 once the store grows long enough that
redundancy, contradiction, or context collapse appear — appending stops scaling and the editing policy becomes
load-bearing. Prefer H02 when the guidance must be shared across agents, models, or users as a maintained artifact.

## Tactical failure mode
The signature failure is porting the attempt → reflect → store → retrieve loop into a new vertical (web
navigation, database querying, embodied navigation, customer service, attack generation, clinical deliberation,
simulated markets) and reporting it as continual learning, without showing that lessons transfer beyond the
environment that produced them or that the gain survives a budget-matched baseline. Rejected members' own stated
limits point the same way: environment-specific experience, online learning from earlier items of the evaluation
benchmark, distillation by a single strong proprietary model, comparisons across mismatched model sizes and
budgets, and a small public evaluation set. The root assumption that fails is that text appended to context is
learning by default — it counts as learning only when its effect is separated from extra compute and from
exposure to the test stream.

## Lessons
### From accepted work
- Contrasting failed with successful trajectories yields insights that complement, rather than duplicate,
  retrieval of successful demonstrations.
- Repairing failed self-play turns and pairing them with successful ones yields situation-specific guidance
  that says both what works and what does not.
- Lessons can be built without ground-truth labels if self-judged outcomes are contrasted and the imperfection of
  the proxy is stated.
- Making task categories and experience transfer discovered rather than predefined lets the store scale with an
  open-ended task stream.
- A retained-artifact ablation — loop on, store removed — is the one piece of evidence accepted members almost
  always supply.
### From rejected work
- Composing modular abstract concepts is a sound idea, but it needs a feedback gate against flawed concepts and an
  evaluation larger than a small public set.
- Group-contrast "semantic advantages" over a frozen model invite budget and model-size confounds; match them.
- Online learning from earlier items of the evaluation benchmark is not evidence of continual learning.
- Explicit prompting for self-assessment or metacognition is not an experience-abstraction mechanism by itself.
