# H10 — Rewrite a Document from Diagnosed Errors
_parent: **Attribute Failures and Patch the Incumbent** (`attribute_and_patch_incumbent`) · corpus support: 55 papers (accepted 14 · rejected 25 · unlabeled 16)_

## Tactical pattern
Hold one textual artifact (a prompt, instruction set, rule document, rubric or tool description) as the
incumbent and rewrite it from error analysis: collect failures and, crucially, successes; have a critic
verbalize what went wrong as a "textual gradient" or a reusable strategy; generate candidate rewrites and
keep the best. The decisive design choice is not the critique but the *selection and retention rule*:
how evaluation budget is spent across candidates (beam, bandit, tree search, score predictors, momentum
over historical candidates) and how earlier wins are protected (atomic or section-level edits, accepting
only individually improving edits, preservation checks on previously correct examples). Success is
certified by a score on a split disjoint from both the critique data and the selection data, not by the
best candidate picked after looking at test results.

## Step-by-Step
1. **Collect both sides.** Analyze successes with failures; failure-only critique causes regressions on
   previously correct examples and overspecializes to the error set.
2. **Aggregate before editing.** Cluster or summarize critiques across failures; per-example critiques
   produce over-specific candidates and costly evaluations.
3. **Localize the edit.** Edit atomic units, sections or rules, and gate each change on a validation split
   disjoint from the critique data; small training sets with no validation split and unstable iterations
   are what sinks several rejected members.
4. **Spend evaluation deliberately.** Allocate candidate scoring with best-arm identification, tree search
   or a learned score predictor, and report optimizer API cost, which can dominate wall-clock time.
5. **Report without test selection.** Do not maxpool over a final beam, report best of several runs, or
   choose the prompt by test performance; accepted members concede exactly these as limitations.

## Differentiation within parent
H10 rewrites *one* document from its own critique. H09 splits credit across several interacting
components; H11 targets reusable skill files and distinguishes genuine skill defects from execution
lapses before editing; H12 edits executable harness code. Outside the parent, prompt-population search
(H13) relies on undirected mutation, whereas H10's edits are directed by a diagnosis.

## When to pick this one
The tell: most behavior lives in one artifact, failures can be verbalized, and labeled or verifiable
examples exist for a disjoint validation split. If the artifact is a skill reused across tasks, H11's
defect-vs-lapse attribution protects working guidance. If failures sit in routing, state or tool
dispatch, text-only rewriting cannot reach them; use H12. If only a scalar score exists and no diagnosis
is possible, population search (H13) is the honest choice.

## Tactical failure mode
The root assumption is that a rewrite which addresses the targeted errors is a net improvement. It fails
through overspecialization to failures, oscillation or stagnation under immediate feedback, judge bias
and judge weakness (guidance underperforms when the evaluator is weaker), overfitting to sample questions
with longer optimization, and prompt saturation on easy tasks. A second, reporting-level failure is
selecting the final artifact with test data, which makes the headline number an upper bound.

## Lessons
### From accepted work
- Concentrate evaluation budget on promising rewrites instead of scoring every candidate fully.
- Preserve wins: feed positive and negative experience, and accept only individually improving edits.
- Carry momentum by sampling strong historical candidates rather than concatenating the whole history.
- Treat interfaces such as tool descriptions as optimizable, verify by execution, and stop adaptively;
  excessive rewriting adds redundancy and overfitting.
- Let the optimization data grow with the artifact so newly emerging weaknesses get exposed.
### From rejected work
- The common rejected shape is the critique-rewrite loop transplanted to a new application (reasoning
  brevity, attack generation, decision-tree splits, image-generation control) with the loop itself unchanged.
- Small training sets without validation splits produced unstable optimization across iterations.
- Guards alone do not carry a paper: section-level edits and preservation tests appear on the rejected side too.
- Dependence on labeled data plus a strong expert feedback model, with validation-based early stopping,
  leaves the improvement claim hard to separate from selection.
- Tweaking only how the critique is computed (response-history similarity, sample-state-aware search,
  syntactic targets) did not separate itself from the crowd.
