# H18 — Co-Evolve Roles and Topology at Test Time
_parent: **Search the Agent's Architecture** (`search_agent_architecture`) · corpus support: 14 papers (accepted 3 · rejected 1 · unlabeled 10)_

## Tactical pattern
During inference the multi-agent team is itself the object being edited: role prompts are generated,
rewritten or retired, edges are added or pruned, and routers or teammate profiles are reweighted from task
feedback, all without touching backbone weights. The decisive design choice is a two-rate update with a
retention gate. Fast, local capability updates (trajectory memories, targeted role-prompt repair) run every
episode; slower, bounded structural edits run only when local repair stalls; verified specialists are cached
and reused across instances so per-query adaptation does not throw away accumulated expertise. The best
members also keep a retreat path: a verifier that may prefer a simple system's answer over the evolved one,
or escalation to deeper collaboration only on stagnation. Success is certified by per-instance or
per-stream gains over both static teams and offline-searched designs, judged by an outcome signal that is
independent of the LLM proposing the edits.

## Step-by-Step
1. Declare what may change (role inventory, edges, routing weights, profiles) and what must be protected
   (answer-format and coordination contracts, required capabilities, a minimal backbone). Unprotected role
   pools drift and lose capabilities the task still needs.
2. Pick the signal that gates structural edits and validate it: task pass/fail or a verifier beats an LLM
   judge, and a judge must be shown to track external success. Steering roles by linguistic or embedding
   proxies of role quality instead of measured competence is what sank the one rejected member.
3. Separate rates: fast memory or prompt updates per trajectory, slow bounded topology edits, and persist an
   edit only when it measurably improves the outcome; cache specialists that pass verification.
4. Build in reversion: let the system fall back to a simpler design or shallower collaboration whenever the
   evolved structure does not win.
5. Account for adaptation compute in total (evolution tokens and inter-agent communication, not just equal
   team size), tune hyperparameters off the evaluation stream, bound archive growth, and test a second
   backbone and a cross-benchmark transfer.

## Differentiation within parent
H18 adapts structure online, per instance or per stream. H17 (Search an Offline Agent Design Space)
searches before deployment and ships one frozen design per task distribution. Adjacent parent: H09
propagates textual feedback through a compound system without necessarily changing its structure.

## When to pick this one
The tell: instances differ in how they should be decomposed, labeled validation data is scarce or absent,
or tasks arrive as a stream where specialist expertise can accumulate. Prefer H17 when the distribution is
stable, an offline search budget exists, and deployment latency or cost must stay flat. Prefer H09 when the
wiring is adequate and only component behavior underperforms.

## Tactical failure mode
Root assumption that fails: that the editing LLM's own assessments (judge scores, self-diagnosis, proxy
metrics of role clarity or complementarity) point reliably toward improvement; convergence arguments in
this sub-pattern explicitly assume the meta-LLM's edits are biased toward beneficial. The rejected member
optimized roles against linguistic and embedding proxies for competence. Unlabeled members show the same
gap from the evidence side: workflow judges whose correlation with external success is unvalidated, noisy
LLM-judge feedback, single runs, hyperparameters tuned on the evaluation benchmark, routing that degrades
across benchmarks, evolution sets of only around twenty examples, archives that grow without limit or are
implemented but never exercised, and a verifier that accepts errors grounded in retrieved content. A second
root assumption is that test-time adaptation is free; several members leave its compute unmatched.

## Evidence profile
Only 2 members carry an `evidence` block (both accepted), too few to profile: held-out separation yes 1,
no 1; optimizer cost yes 1, unclear 1; cross-task, variance, budget matching, retained-artifact ablation,
improver comparison, leakage guard and code are unclear in both. Counting instead over the
`limitations_evident` lists of all 14 members: 3 flag incomplete compute or cost matching, 3 flag split or
tuning hygiene (evaluation-tuned hyperparameters, deferred or unestablished splits), 3 flag transfer limits
across benchmarks or backbones, 2 flag a single backbone, and 2 flag unvalidated or noisy judges. Typical
report: gains on the adapted stream. Typical omission: matched adaptation compute and an independent split.

## Lessons
### From accepted work
- Let roles and edges emerge from local experience: per-agent trajectory memories specialize routing and
  execution, while success-weighted edge updates prune collaborations that do not pay.
- Design per instance without labels by critiquing subtask solvability and completeness, and let a
  verifier choose between the evolved team and a simple one; the right to revert keeps complexity honest.
- Refuse the choice between a frozen workflow and rebuilding per query: retain discovered specialists as
  state and escalate to structural change only when retained structure falls short.
- Gate every structural change on a task-outcome signal rather than on the proposer's self-assessment.
### From rejected work
- Evolving profiles against linguistic or embedding proxies for role quality, rather than measured task
  competence, leaves the central claim unverified.
- Decentralized coordination that still leans on auxiliary middleware for environment interaction weakens
  the claim that the team adapts itself.
- With one rejected member, treat these as cautions, not a pattern.
