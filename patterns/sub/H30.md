# H30 — Govern Library Admission and Retirement
_parent: **Gate Retention on Measured Evidence** (`evidence_gated_retention`) · corpus support: 12 papers (accepted 2 · rejected 1 · unlabeled 9)_

## Tactical pattern
A shared skill or experience library is treated as a governed population, not an append-only log. A candidate
entry earns membership only after passing attribution (did this sub-step actually cause the success?),
admissibility checks, a marginal-gain or counterfactual replay, and a regression budget on previously solved
cases; it is quarantined until then. Existing entries are retired, merged or deleted when evidence of harm or
low observed utility accumulates, the library has a hard size bound, and every change is versioned with
provenance so it can be rolled back. The decisive design choice is pairing the admission gate with a retirement
rule: either alone lets the collection degrade silently. Success is certified when the governed library beats both a no-skill
baseline and an ungoverned accumulating library on held-out tasks, at a stated call cost.

## Step-by-Step
1. Log per-task outcomes and attribute success or failure to the specific entries and sub-steps used; a whole
   successful trajectory does not authorize an update by itself.
2. Hold new entries in quarantine and replay them on a balanced probe that includes cases already solved, with a
   hard regression budget; promote only on net fixes.
3. Check sets, not just singles: entries interact, and a contaminated entry can poison later distillation even
   after it is deleted, so test the marginal gain of the subset that will be injected.
4. Bound library size and retire conservatively on accumulated evidence of harm or disuse; record versions and
   provenance for every add, edit, merge and retirement.
5. Evaluate on a frozen library against held-out tasks, with seeds and the extra call cost. Versioning and
   registering every refined component without an admission test or a retirement rule is what sank the rejected
   member; letting held-out tests participate in promotion is the most common unlabeled leak.

## Differentiation within parent
H30 manages membership of a shared library. H29 ranks or demotes entries by learned value but need not remove any.
H31 decides whether a single incumbent (the whole agent, harness or policy) is replaced, often with formal or
anytime-valid guarantees; H30's gates are per-entry and operate inside a population.

## When to pick this one
The tell: a growing collection of reusable skills, where retrieval quality or task success falls as the
collection grows, or where one new entry fixes a failure while breaking earlier cases. Pick H29 if the store can
stay unbounded and only ranking is wrong; pick H31 if there is one artifact and the question is whether to commit
an edit to it.

## Tactical failure mode
The root assumption that fails is that a plausible, successful-looking entry is a net positive for the library.
The rejected member versioned tools, environments and agents under one protocol and let feedback refine them, but
had no measured admission or retirement step, so its limitations are protocol complexity and overhead rather than
evidence. Unlabeled members fail differently: held-out tests join promotion, online task streams stand in for
frozen-library evaluation, leave-one-out attribution is too expensive to repeat, gains are heavy-tailed with
negative transfer, LLM validators miss subtle quality problems, and gating splits are small with no repeated
evolution seeds.

## Lessons
### From accepted work
- Name the silent failure first (degraded retrieval and harmful injected guidance as the library grows), then show
  governance fixes it.
- Combine a prior on how entries are authored with conservative retirement and a bounded library.
- Report the cost of governance against a no-skill baseline, even when it is a large overhead.
- Use successful memories to condition the offline skill evolver instead of enlarging every inference prompt;
  deduplicate and prune by utility to control growth.
- Admit that governance settings depend on library scale.
### From rejected work
- A lifecycle or versioning protocol is infrastructure, not evidence; without a measured admission or retirement
  test the retention claim is unsupported.
- When every evidence field is unclear, reviewers cannot separate the protocol's effect from the backbone's.
- Listing complexity and communication overhead as the main limitations signals that benefits were not isolated.
