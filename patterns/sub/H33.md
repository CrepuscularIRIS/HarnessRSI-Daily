# H33 — Benchmark Persistent Improvement over Streams
_parent: **Diagnose Whether Self-Improvement Is Real** (`diagnose_self_improvement`) · corpus support: 18 papers (accepted 0 · rejected 3 · unlabeled 15)_

## Tactical pattern
Replace single-episode success with an *ordered stream* of tasks. The benchmark can then tell apart an agent that
retains and reuses experience from one that just re-solves each task with expensive fresh reasoning. The decisive
design choice is the measurement views around the stream: a frozen snapshot of the retained artifact, a validation
view separate from the update signal, held-out or shifted test views (in-distribution and out-of-distribution), a
replay view for forgetting, and cost accounting per step. A further choice is how splits guarantee that relevant
support exists on the training side, so a failure on held-out tasks means poor encoding, routing, or uptake rather
than a task the stream never taught. Success is certified when the frozen artifact transfers, persists without
regression, and lowers cost at matched success, not when the in-situ curve rises.

## Step-by-Step
1. Define the persistence claim being measured (accumulation, transfer, forgetting, belief revision, or cost
   reduction), and pick an ordering that makes later tasks depend on earlier ones.
2. Freeze the retained artifact before testing. In-situ stream scores mix ongoing search with retained improvement.
   Leaving out the freeze is the gap the rejected members leave open.
3. Build disjoint views: permissive exploration vs. strict evaluation, ID vs. OOD, a replay view for regressions.
   Make sure held-out tasks still have procedural support in the training stream.
4. Add controls that defeat shortcuts: raw-trajectory baselines against distilled abstractions, strong
   long-context baselines against explicit memory, obfuscated identifiers against prior knowledge, and controlled
   signal-and-noise failure histories against self-generated ones.
5. Record cost and run-to-run variation next to accuracy, and report when the harness itself fails. Keep the
   benchmark separate from any method you propose; bolting a new method onto the benchmark blurs the contribution.

## Differentiation within parent
H33 measures whether improvement *persists and transfers*. H34 measures whether an agent can *carry out* the
improvement work (build pipelines, harnesses, learning code). H32 analyzes one existing system with targeted
interventions instead of releasing a reusable benchmark. H35 asks whether persistence carries harm, not benefit.

## When to pick this one
The tell is a field that reports per-episode success for systems whose whole claim is cross-episode learning.
Another tell is equal success rates hiding very different execution cost. Pick H33 when the missing piece is a
shared yardstick. If a single system's gain needs explaining, H32 is cheaper. If the object under test is the
agent's research ability, use H34.

## Tactical failure mode
The root assumption that fails is that ordering tasks into a stream is, by itself, enough to measure persistence.
Unlabeled members' limitations name the leaks: reuse evaluated on the same tasks that produced the retained
artifacts, analysis restricted to tasks the producer already solved, overall improvement smaller than
run-to-run variation, harness failures that leave results inconclusive, model rankings that shift on retest,
splits selected for headroom, and contamination from pretraining. The rejected members show a second failure.
They frame evaluation around a normative ideal (agents *should* be tested on lifelong or proactive learning)
and headline that current models are deficient, while one of them also attaches a new self-consistency
method. A third (low fit) generalizes a learning-method selector whose gains shrink as environments and
methods grow.

## Evidence profile
No member of H33 carries an `evidence` block (n = 0), so no reporting rates are given. Qualitatively, from
mechanisms and limitations: the strongest unlabeled designs freeze snapshots, separate ID/OOD/replay views, and
record evolution costs. Recurring omissions are repeated-seed uncertainty, more than one environment family, and
excluding the producing tasks from reuse evaluation. Each of the 3 rejected members lists only one limitation.

## Lessons
### From accepted work
- No H33 member is labeled accepted. The bullets below are design features of unlabeled members, not acceptance evidence.
- Evaluate frozen snapshots under separate validation, ID, OOD, and replay views without changing native update rules.
- Pair distilled skills with raw-trajectory controls so procedural abstraction is not confused with episodic reuse.
- Guarantee train-side procedural support for each held-out split, so failures can be localized to encoding,
  routing, or uptake.
- Measure whether retained experience cuts execution cost at maintained success, not only whether success rises.
### From rejected work
- A normative case that agents should be tested on lifelong learning does not substitute for a frozen, held-out
  transfer view.
- Shipping a benchmark together with a new method splits the contribution, and neither part is evaluated cleanly.
- Headlining that current models fail long-term memory tests, without cost or variance accounting, reads as
  descriptive rather than diagnostic.
- Generality claims for adaptive method selection are undercut when gains diminish as the method space grows.
