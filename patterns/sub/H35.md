# H35 — Audit Persistence as an Attack Surface
_parent: **Diagnose Whether Self-Improvement Is Real** (`diagnose_self_improvement`) · corpus support: 13 papers (accepted 3 · rejected 3 · unlabeled 7)_

## Tactical pattern
Treat the persistent state that makes self-improvement possible (memory, skills, harness edits, evolving tools) as
a channel through which harm *outlives the session that caused it*. The move is to introduce poisoning,
injection, tampering, or plain benign misevolution into that state. The harm is then traced across sessions and
generations: whether it persists, propagates through imitation or reuse, and evades defenses that inspect one entry
or one session at a time. The decisive design choice is to tie the threat to the *update dynamics* (what gets
written, consolidated, imitated, or accepted as a fix) rather than to a one-shot prompt. Success is certified by a
paired comparison against a non-evolving or pre-evolution counterpart, with the threat model and the scope of
mitigation stated explicitly.

## Step-by-Step
1. Map which modules persist and at which lifecycle stage each can be written, retrieved, consolidated, or copied.
   That gives a module-by-lifecycle attack surface.
2. Choose the perturbation by where it enters. An adversarial item or record enters via the memory update.
   Benign self-evolution drifts on its own. A planted nonexistent failure invites an unwarranted fix.
3. Run paired conditions: evolved vs. non-evolving, before vs. after evolution, tampered vs. benign edits matched
   within the same diffs. Freeze the state before probing.
4. Trace persistence over time, through session resets, context truncation, and removal of the original plant,
   and test defenses that check single entries or single sessions.
5. State the threat model and its ceiling: editable inputs assumed, number of seeds, frameworks or providers
   covered, and how much of the safety the mitigation restores. Stopping at one injected record, or validating
   memory against reasoning paths drawn from that same memory, leaves propagation across generations unexamined.

## Differentiation within parent
H35 is the only safety- and adversary-focused sibling. H32, H33, and H34 ask whether improvement is real or
measurable as capability. H35 asks what else persistence carries forward. It shares H32's paired-control logic,
but the outcome measured is unauthorized behavior, alignment drift, or integrity violation, not task gain.

## When to pick this one
The tell is a system that writes its own durable state from untrusted or self-generated inputs and is evaluated
only per session. Another tell is a harness optimizer that is allowed to add guardrails or edits without checking
whether they are warranted. Pick H35 when the risk lies in what persists. If you only doubt that the gain is
genuine, H32 is the right tool.

## Tactical failure mode
The root assumption that fails is that a static, entry-level or single-session evaluation reflects the risk of an
evolving system. Attacks and defenses built on that assumption fall short in two ways. Some show that one
injected record redirects later answers but never trace propagation across generations. Others propose
consensus-style memory self-validation whose reasoning paths come from the memory it is meant to check. Recurring limitations elsewhere:
evaluation on a single seed, stealth measured only through proxies, a single micro-lab, mitigations that only
partly restore safety, seeded tampering that may not match natural tampering, judges sharing a model with the
optimizer, and threat taxonomies broader than their empirical coverage.

## Lessons
### From accepted work
- Exploit the *update* path. Optimizing triggers jointly with a memory-corruption strategy beats static text
  attacks on continually updated memory.
- Audit safety longitudinally and before/after evolution across several pathways (parameters, memory, tools,
  workflow). Utility gains do not imply safety retention.
- Probe proposed fixes with a deterministic nonexistent failure. Edits that suppress nothing can still be
  unwarranted fabrications.
- Bound the claim in writing: single seed, editable-input threat model, partial mitigation, single micro-lab.
- Pair each attack with a mitigation or acceptance rule, even one that only partly restores safety.
### From rejected work
- A single-interaction poisoning result without cross-session or cross-generation tracing leaves the persistence
  claim unexamined.
- A consensus-validation defense built on memory-derived reasoning paths must show it holds when the poison
  reaches most of those paths.
- Showing alignment drift only in controllable testbeds, while noting defenses stay fragile, leaves the
  deployment-level claim open.
