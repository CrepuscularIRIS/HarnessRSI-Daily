arXiv: 2611.22432    title: Think Once, Act Fast: Cost-Effective Recursive Self-Improvement for Decision Model Harness

[abstract]
Fast System-1 models enable responsive actions but struggle with complex or unfamiliar tasks. Large language model (LLM)-based System-2 provides strong reasoning capabilities, yet repeated invocation incurs substantial latency and computational costs. To bridge this gap, we propose JevRSI, a System-1/System-2 collaboration framework that transforms LLM reasoning into transferable decision support through recursive harness improvement. A Jev-style System-1 model enables low-latency action selection, while an asynchronous System-2 LLM develops a modular harness. The harness integrates procedures, state memory, and world models to provide decision contexts spanning the past, present, and future. Utility-driven retrieval selects modules based on both contextual relevance and their utility on previous tasks. Environmental feedback guides targeted revisions and enables recursive self-improvement. Experiments across three datasets show that JevRSI consistently improves decision quality, matching or surpassing LLM agents in several settings. It preserves low-latency execution with favorable performance-cost trade-offs. Moreover, System-2 development interventions decrease as the harness evolves, amortizing System-2 costs through continuous improvement. Our project is available at https://github.com/usail-hkust/JevRSI.

[intro/head from cached fulltext]
Developing agents that act efficiently and accurately across diverse environments remains a fundamental challenge in artificial intelligence. Large language models (LLMs) offer broad knowledge and powerful reasoning capabilities, but their computational overhead and latency hinder time-sensitive applications and tasks ( e.g. embodied agents, traffic signal control ( Lai et al., 2025 ) ). Existing approaches follow three paradigms. System-1 agents employ lightweight models, including rule-based systems, small LLMs, and vision-language-action (VLA) models ( Kim et al., 2024 ) . They successfully enable fast decision-making but often struggle with complex or unfamiliar tasks. System-2 agents leverage larger LLMs for deliberate reasoning, offering great flexibility with extensive inference cost ( Yao et al., 2022 ; Prasad et al., 2023 ) . System-1/System-2 cooperative methods combine these strengths by selectively invoking sophisticated reasoning while delegating routine decisions to fast executors, aiming to balance decision quality and execution efficiency ( Lin et al., 2023 ; NVIDIA et al., 2025 ) . However, existing System-1/System-2 collaboration methods primarily focus on allocating computation by selecting when to invoke System-1 or System-2 ( Lin et al., 2023 ) . Each System-2 invocation still incurs substantially greater latency. This motivates distilling LLM reasoning into reusable support for System-1 while preserving its responsiveness. Jev-style ( Almeida, 2026 ) decision models offer a promising paradigm for this goal. Its structured probabilistic decision interface supports fast, high-quality decision-making and strong generalization, making it well suited to System-1 execution. To equip decision models with LLM reasoning capabilities, we propose recursively improving its harness, constructing informative decision context and guide action selection. LLM-based System-2 leverages interaction feedback to revise the harness, allowing recursive self-improvement to support subsequent decisions. Through this cycle, we aim to continually improve decision quality while preserving the responsiveness of System-1 execution. Achieving this goal requires addressing two coupled challenges. First, the agent need to accurately identify System-1 failures and determine when to invoke LLM reasoning for harness revision. Delayed intervention may compromise decision quality, while excessive intervention introduces substantial computational overhead. Second, the agent need to leverage real-time environmental feedback to drive continual harness improvement. This requires to accurately identify the underlying harness deficiencies and making targeted improvements to address them. As the harness evolves, the agent continuously diagnoses emerging limitations and refine its decision context accordingly. Together, timely intervention and feedback-driven improvement enable the harness to progressively improve decision quality while preserving the responsiveness of System-1 execution. To address these challenges, we propose JevRSI , a System-1/System-2 collaboration framework that enables fast execution with recursive harness improvement. During execution, a Jev-style decision model serves as System-1, supporting low-latency decisions across tasks and environments. Utility-driven retrieval selects most suitable harness modules to provide informative decision context. Meanwhile, System-2 develops three complementary harness modules that provide System-1 with decision contexts spanning the past, present, and future. (1) State memory retains exploration history and observed transitions. It is constructed by a task-specific memory-building program for different objectives. (2) Procedure encodes experience to guide the current action under applicable environmental conditions and task goals. (3) World model is a predictive model to anticipate future environmental states and action outcomes, enabling counterfactual evaluation of candidate actions. System-2 uses feedback from ongoing execution to diagnose gaps in these modules and make targeted revisions. Through this recursive development process, JevRSI progressively enhances decision quality and reduces dependence on System-2 with the responsiveness of System-1 execution. Our contributions are threefold: (1) We propose JevRSI, the first framework that enables recursive self-improvement of decision-model harnesses during execution, supporting efficient and generalizable decision-making across diverse tasks and environments. (2) We design a modular harness that integrates exploration history, action guidance, and counterfactual predictions to provide System-1 with informative past, present, and future decision contexts. Feedback-driven harness development further enables continual self-improvement and transfer across execution episodes.
