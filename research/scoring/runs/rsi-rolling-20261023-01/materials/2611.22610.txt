arXiv: 2611.22610    title: EvoSkill-S1: Evolving Reusable Skills with Genetic-Inspired Operators

[abstract]
Reusable skills allow language-model agents to accumulate and reuse problem-solving experience. However, most existing methods improve a skill bank only indirectly through rollout-driven accumulation and heuristic reflection, often resulting in shallow and weakly controlled updates. We present EvoSkill-S1, an RL-driven framework that instead treats reusable skills themselves as the direct objects of evolution. Given hard-negative failures, EvoSkill-S1 selects parent skills and transforms them into candidate child skills through five genetic-inspired operators: revision, cross-revision, mutation, dual-reflection, and crossover. Downstream task rewards train the skill evolver to generate more effective child skills, while evaluator-guided write-back selectively retains beneficial updates. Complementary Skill Bank Fusion consolidates operator-specific banks to exploit their distinct recovery patterns, while a Procedural Skill Interface supports transfer to unseen problems. Across all reported math and code benchmarks, EvoSkill-S1 improves the corresponding initialized skill banks under both Qwen and GPT-family solvers, with relative gains of up to 12.39%. Compared with the strongest external baseline without direct skill evolution, the largest relative improvement reaches 20.39%.

[intro/head from cached fulltext]
Large language models (LLMs) have become increasingly capable of complex reasoning, including mathematical problem solving and code generation. Nevertheless, many challenging problems still benefit from reusable problem-solving experience rather than isolated one-shot generation ( Brown et al., 2020 ; Wei et al., 2022 ; Kojima et al., 2022 ; Wang et al., 2023 ; Cobbe et al., 2021 ; Hendrycks et al., 2021b ; Chen et al., 2021 ; Li et al., 2022 ) . A natural way to provide such experience is through a skill bank: a collection of compact abstractions, strategies, and cautions that can be retrieved for related problems, echoing broader work on language-agent memory, tool use, and experience reuse ( Yao et al., 2023 ; Schick et al., 2023 ; Park et al., 2023 ; Wang et al., 2024 ; Packer et al., 2023 ; Shinn et al., 2023 ; Zhao et al., 2024 ) . Many existing approaches improve such banks only indirectly. As new rollouts arrive, they append new skills or summarize and reflect on additional experience ( Wang et al., 2024 ; Shinn et al., 2023 ; Zhao et al., 2024 ) . This rollout-driven maintenance supports continual accumulation, but often leaves existing skills largely unchanged or only shallowly edited through weakly controlled heuristics. Consequently, a skill bank may continue to grow without systematically repairing brittle strategies, exploring alternative solution routes, or combining complementary partial skills. We instead ask whether reusable skills themselves can become the direct objects of evolution. In math and code, unresolved rollout failures provide concrete diagnostic signals: they expose where retrieved skills remain incomplete, brittle, or poorly matched to the target problem ( Cobbe et al., 2021 ; Lightman et al., 2024 ; Chen et al., 2021 ; Austin et al., 2021 ; Hendrycks et al., 2021a ; Jain et al., 2025 ) . Rather than merely collecting more experience from these failures, they can be used to directly transform existing skills into stronger descendants. Direct skill-level evolution, however, is non-trivial. First, different failure modes require different transformations: some skills need conservative repair, while others require cross-problem transfer, an alternative solution route, stronger guardrails, or composition with another skill. Second, a single hard negative can yield multiple plausible child skills, requiring a principled mechanism for comparing candidates and assigning credit to useful transformations. Third, evolved skills may retain artifacts specific to their source problems, limiting transfer to unseen problems. Finally, different operators may recover complementary subsets of failures, requiring a mechanism to consolidate their distinct strengths. These challenges lead to the following question: Can reusable skills themselves be directly and iteratively evolved into stronger descendants while remaining efficient enough for skill-bank improvement? In response, we propose EvoSkill-S1 , an RL-driven framework for direct skill-level evolution. Without updating the base solver, EvoSkill-S1 uses hard negatives as evolution targets, selects parent and support skills from the current bank, and transforms them into candidate child skills through five genetic-inspired operators: revision, cross-revision, mutation, dual-reflection, and crossover. Using GRPO -style group-relative feedback, the trainable skill evolver learns to favor child skills that improve downstream task performance, while evaluator-guided write-back retains beneficial updates and discards ineffective or redundant candidates. Because different operators exhibit complementary repair patterns, Complementary Skill Bank Fusion consolidates their operator-specific banks under the same overall skill-bank budget. A Procedural Skill Interface further supports transfer of evolved skills to unseen problems. Experiments across math and code benchmarks show that EvoSkill-S1 consistently improves the corresponding initialized skill banks under both Qwen and GPT-family solvers, achieving relative gains of up to 12.39%. Compared with the strongest external baseline without direct skill evolution, the largest relative improvement reaches 20.39%. This paper makes three main contributions: • RL-guided direct skill evolution. We formulate skill-bank improvement as an explicit optimization problem over reusable skills themselves and train a dedicated skill evolver with downstream task rewards to transform existing parent skills into stronger child skills, while keeping the base solver fixed. • Complementary genetic-inspired operators. We introduce five genetic-inspired operators that apply distinct transformations to reusable skills. Their complementary recovery patterns address heterogeneous failure modes and motivate the fusion of operator-specific skill banks. • Complementary Skill Bank Fusion and empirical validation.
