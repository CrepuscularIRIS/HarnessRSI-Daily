arXiv: 2611.24924    title: Agentic Harness Engineering Beta: Requirement-Centered Evolution of Agent Harnesses

[abstract]
Real-world harness engineering is driven by explicit and implicit user requirements, which guide development and validation. However, existing harness evolution methods often overlook implicit requirements and interaction needs, while using a single evolution agent to modify the harness and benchmark scores to select changes. This process often produces narrow patches and leads to benchmark-specific local optima. We introduce Agentic Harness Engineering Beta (AHE-Beta), a requirement-centered loop that brings real-world engineering practices into harness evolution. The loop consists of three steps: (1) User-driven evaluation lets simulated users raise their requirements as issues while they work with the harness over multiple turns. (2) Team-based modification implements those requirements as generalizable changes rather than task-specific patches. (3) Requirement-driven selection merges a change only when it resolves a verified requirement and passes review. Empirically, on held-out tasks, AHE-Beta raises pass@1 from 21.9% to 33.3% while using 40.8% fewer tokens than Base Harness, and achieves higher pass@1 than Claude Code, Meta-Harness, and AHE. In a blind human A/B test, real users prefer AHE-Beta in 31 of 48 valid pairs and rate it higher on all five dimensions, matching the directional preference of the user simulator. The human A/B results show that AHE-Beta achieves its goal of aligning harness evolution with real-world user requirements. Without further evolution, the gains transfer to GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash with pass@1 improvements of 15.8% and 5.3% over Base Harness. Together, these results show that requirement-centered harness evolution is a more general method for improving agent capabilities.

Coding agents are increasingly deployed on long-horizon software-engineering tasks, including issue resolution in real-world repositories and multi-step terminal workflows [ 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 ] . Performance on such tasks depends not only on the model but also on the harness, which comprises the prompts, tools, and middleware around a base model and shapes how the model executes tasks and interacts with users. For a fixed model, behavior varies substantially across harness designs [ 6 , 7 ] , and effective designs are model-specific [ 8 , 9 ] , this model dependence motivates harness evolution. Real-world harness engineering is driven by user requirements, which can be explicit or implicit. Explicit requirements are stated in user requests, while implicit requirements are unstated expectations about the results or behavior of the harness. Engineering teams use both to guide development and validation. Recent harness evolution methods, however, center on benchmark outcomes [ 10 , 11 , 8 ] , leaving gaps between these methods and real-world engineering practice in evaluation, modification, and selection. In evaluation, benchmark rollouts produce execution trajectories and verifier results for predefined tasks. These records can miss implicit user requirements, including unstated preferences about scope and cost, expectations revealed through mid-run corrections, and the need for timely responses and visible verification evidence [ 12 , 13 ] . In modification, an evolution agent can improve benchmark scores by fixing observed failures without explicitly defining the user requirements behind those fixes [ 10 , 11 , 8 ] . Real-world harness engineering instead judges changes by whether they resolve user problems, so higher benchmark scores alone do not establish that a change is useful. In selection, benchmark scores alone cannot distinguish general fixes from task-specific patches. Score-based selection can therefore reinforce overfitting and trap evolution in benchmark-specific local optima [ 14 , 15 ] . The key challenge in agent-driven harness evolution is to produce changes that satisfy both explicit and implicit user requirements while generalizing beyond the evolution tasks. Our central insight is that simulated user interactions can reveal implicit requirements that task verifiers miss and provide evidence of whether harness behavior satisfies user needs. These requirements can guide general fixes across related failures and provide acceptance criteria independent of benchmark scores. We propose Agentic Harness Engineering Beta (AHE-Beta), a requirement-centered loop that brings real-world engineering practices into each of the three steps. ❶ User-driven evaluation draws feedback from multi-turn sessions in which the user simulator works with the current harness and files issues for requirements that task verifiers miss. ❷ Team-based modification hands these issues to a Gitea-based team in which Triage filters out invalid and task-specific ones, the Architect merges those sharing a root cause into consolidated requirements, the Product Manager (PM) infers requirements that users leave unstated, and the Developers turn them into general fixes. ❸ Requirement-driven selection ties each merge to a verified requirement and an approved review, and reserves benchmark scores for evaluation after evolution. We evaluate AHE-Beta on held-out tasks from Harbor-index [ 16 ] . Compared with Base Harness, AHE-Beta raises pass@1 from 21.9% to 33.3% and user rating from 2.59 to 3.04, while using 40.8% fewer tokens and exceeding Claude Code [ 17 ] , Meta-Harness [ 11 ] , and AHE [ 10 ] in pass@1. In a blind human A/B test, real users prefer AHE-Beta in 31 of 48 valid pairs and rate it higher on all five dimensions. Without further evolution, pass@1 also improves by 15.8 and 5.3 percentage points over Base Harness on GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, respectively. These transfer results indicate that selecting harness modifications according to user requirements can produce improvements that generalize beyond the tasks and model used during evolution. We also find that some verifier-passing sessions never deliver a completed reply and receive the minimum user rating, showing that pass@1 alone is an incomplete measure of requirement satisfaction. Together, these results show that user requirements can guide improvements in task execution, efficiency, and real-user experience without selecting changes by benchmark score. This paper makes two contributions: • We propose AHE-Beta, a harness evolution framework centered on user requirements. It combines user-driven evaluation, team-based modification, and requirement-driven selection to identify unmet needs, develop general fixes, and merge changes that resolve verified requirements. • We show that requirement-centered harness evolution improves pass@1, user rating, and token efficiency on held-out tasks.
