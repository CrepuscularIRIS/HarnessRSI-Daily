arXiv: 2611.27689    title: Embodied-Harness: Towards Recursive Self-Improvement for Robotic Manipulation

[abstract]
Vision-language model (VLM)-based agents offer a promising approach to robotic manipulation, yet remain limited by inadequate memory organization, uncertain failure attribution, and limited reuse of execution experience. We propose Embodied-Harness, a dual-agent framework that improves manipulation by refining the execution harness without updating model parameters. The Actor interacts with the environment through a robot-control interface, while the Reflector converts execution evidence into reusable operational guidance. Hierarchical Memory Architecture maintains short-term execution context and long-term experiential knowledge to support execution and reflection. Evidence-based Experience Reflection grounds failure diagnosis in observed interactions, while Recursive Harness Self-Improvement refines operational guidance across successive attempts. Experiments on RoboTwin 2.0, RoboSPA, and RMBench demonstrate the effectiveness of Embodied-Harness in generalization under environmental variations, spatial reasoning, long-horizon planning, and memory-dependent manipulation. Our method achieves success rates of 82.4%, 76.7%, and 94.4% on the three benchmarks, respectively. Real-world evaluation further yields an average success rate of 96.7% across three manipulation tasks. These results highlight the potential of Embodied-Harness to improve robotic manipulation through experience-driven harness self-improvement.

Robotic manipulation is a fundamental capability of embodied intelligence Billard and Kragic (2019) ; Kroemer et al. (2021) ; Duan et al. (2022) , enabling robots to interact with the physical world and perform diverse tasks across household assistance Li et al. (2023) ; Fu et al. (2025) , industrial manufacturing Narang et al. (2022) ; Tang et al. (2023) , and healthcare Dupont et al. (2021) ; Kim et al. (2025a) . Developing general manipulation capabilities is essential for enabling versatile and autonomous robotic operation in real-world environments, representing a critical step toward general embodied intelligence (GEI) Firoozi et al. (2025) ; Liu et al. (2025a) ; Team et al. (2025) . Towards this goal, different paradigms have emerged for robotic manipulation. End-to-end frameworks, including vision-language-action (VLA) models Zitkovich et al. (2023) ; Kim et al. (2025b) ; NVIDIA et al. (2025) ; Black et al. (2025a) ; Shukor et al. (2025) ; Zheng et al. (2026) ; Wu et al. (2026) and world-action models (WAMs) Hu et al. (2025a) ; Bi et al. (2026) ; Kim et al. (2026) ; Li et al. (2026a) ; Ye et al. (2026) ; Yuan et al. (2026) , have demonstrated remarkable progress in learning robotic policies from multimodal inputs. However, these approaches often rely on large-scale robotic datasets, face challenges in generalizing to unseen environments O’Neill et al. (2024) ; Khazatsky et al. (2024) ; Ma et al. (2026) , and struggle with reliable long-horizon manipulation Mees et al. (2022) ; Liu et al. (2023a) ; Fan et al. (2026) . In contrast, agentic frameworks leverage the reasoning and planning capabilities of vision-language models (VLMs) to decompose complex tasks and coordinate robot actions ichter et al. (2023) ; Liang et al. (2023) ; Huang et al. (2023a) ; Wang et al. (2026a) . Such frameworks support flexible action planning and feedback-driven execution, offering a promising alternative without requiring task-specific updates to the underlying model parameters Huang et al. (2023b) ; Duan et al. (2025a) ; Zhang et al. (2026) . Despite these advantages, agentic frameworks still face several challenges in achieving reliable robotic manipulation. (i) Inadequate Memory Organization. Many real-world manipulation tasks are inherently long-horizon, requiring agents to track task progress and detailed execution histories while retaining experience across attempts and tasks Park et al. (2023) ; Wang et al. (2024) ; Zhang et al. (2025a) ; Chen et al. (2026a) . Managing such information across different temporal scales and levels of granularity remains challenging. (ii) Uncertain Failure Attribution. Manipulation failures may stem from inaccurate action grounding, unsatisfied preconditions, or violated state constraints Liu et al. (2023b) ; Duan et al. (2025b) ; Xia et al. (2025) . However, execution feedback often reveals only observable outcomes rather than underlying causes, making it difficult to identify critical errors and derive reliable operational guidance. (iii) Limited Self-Improvement Capability. Even when agents identify execution errors and extract useful insights, these reflections may remain isolated from subsequent executions. Without mechanisms to accumulate, validate, and refine operational guidance across attempts, agents may repeatedly encounter similar failures Shinn et al. (2023) ; Zhao et al. (2024) ; Chen et al. (2024) . To address these challenges, we propose Embodied-Harness , a dual-agent framework for robotic manipulation that enables recursive harness self-improvement through accumulated execution experience (Fig. ). The framework comprises two complementary roles, an Actor and a Reflector . The Actor interacts with the environment through control interfaces, while the Reflector analyzes execution trajectories and environmental feedback to improve subsequent task execution. Specifically, Embodied-Harness incorporates three complementary mechanisms: (i) Hierarchical Memory Architecture organizes short-term execution context and long-term experiential knowledge across different temporal scales; (ii) Evidence-based Experience Reflection identifies potential failure causes from execution evidence and derives actionable operational guidance; and (iii) Recursive Harness Self-Improvement refines the execution harness using accumulated guidance, with subsequent execution feedback informing further reflection and refinement. Together, these mechanisms enable continual improvement in robotic manipulation without updating the underlying model parameters. We evaluate Embodied-Harness on three robotic manipulation benchmarks, RoboTwin 2.0, RoboSPA, and RMBench, covering generalization under environmental variations, fine-grained spatial reasoning, long-horizon procedural planning, and memory-dependent manipulation.
