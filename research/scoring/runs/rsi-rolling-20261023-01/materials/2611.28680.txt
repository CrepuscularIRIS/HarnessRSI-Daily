arXiv: 2611.28680    title: Improving the Improver: Semantic PID Control for Medical Agent Self-Improvement

[abstract]
High-stakes vertical medical tasks, such as postoperative pathological staging and long-term survival prediction, demand multi-step clinical reasoning over heterogeneous evidence under weak supervision and high outcome uncertainty. While we first orchestrate CliAgent, a domain-tailored clinical agent workflow that systematically grounds predictions in multi-modal patient evidence, manually designed medical agents remain constrained by static, brittle rules. Moreover, existing recursive self-improvement (RSI) methods suffer from feedback ambiguity and severe development-set overfitting when evolving agents in high-noise medical domains. Focusing on improving the improver, we propose Semantic PID-RSI, an evidence-guided control-theoretic framework for medical agent self-improvement. Inspired by PID control, our improver evaluates candidate modification batches via three diagnostic roles: P isolates current clinical failure modes, I synthesizes longitudinal evidence from past trials, and D estimates response dynamics and regression risks (D-pre/D-post). Selected plans are compiled into behavior contracts for staged probing and independent adoption judging. Instantiated and validated on challenging oncology tasks (pT/pN staging and survival prognosis), evolving CliAgent with Semantic PID-RSI on a 3B-activated open-weight model achieves substantial gains (up to +6.27 C-index points), outperforming overfitting-prone RSI baselines and approaching the performance of frontier proprietary models (e.g., GPT-6 series) equipped with CliAgent.

[intro/head from cached fulltext]
High-stakes vertical medical tasks, exemplified by postoperative pathological staging (e.g., pT and pN staging) and long-term survival prediction in oncology [ 2 , 25 ] , carry profound implications for clinical treatment planning. Unlike routine medical question answering [ 24 , 18 ] , decision-making in specialized clinical domains demands multi-step reasoning over noisy, heterogeneous patient evidence under weak supervision and high outcome uncertainty [ 9 , 4 ] . When applied directly, general-purpose large language models (LLMs) struggle to systematically weigh conflicting clinical indicators [ 6 ] . To structure clinical decision-making beyond raw prompting, building on recent advances in medical agentic workflows [ 26 , 12 , 27 ] , we first orchestrate CliAgent, a domain-tailored clinical agent workflow that systematically grounds predictions in multi-modal patient evidence through structured extraction, rule verification, and risk aggregation, establishing a reliable reasoning foundation across diverse LLM backbones. Despite providing a strong structural prior, manually orchestrated medical agents like CliAgent remain bottlenecked by static, human-crafted aggregation rules that are brittle across complex patient subpopulations. Recursive self-improvement (RSI) and automated agent optimization methods, ranging from verbal self-reflection [ 23 , 15 ] and prompt evolution [ 29 , 10 , 31 ] to executable workflow and program search [ 8 , 33 , 19 , 34 ] , offer a promising paradigm to automatically refine domain prompts and agentic structures using evaluator feedback. However, when evolving medical agents in high-noise clinical environments, the effectiveness of RSI is fundamentally bottlenecked by the quality of the improver itself, exposing two critical failure modes. First, feedback ambiguity : an aggregate scalar score (e.g., AUC or Harrell’s C-index [ 7 ] ) indicates whether performance shifted, but leaves ambiguous which specific clinical reasoning step caused the error and whether a similar edit already failed in earlier rounds. Second, uncontrolled regression and development-set overfitting : without explicit behavioral constraints, existing improvers greedily exploit spurious correlations on the development split while silently breaking valid clinical logic. For example, state-of-the-art RSI frameworks such as OPRO [ 29 ] , Penguin-RSI, and ACE [ 34 ] achieve high development scores (up to 74.35% Avg.) but suffer severe generalization drops of 4.86–5.39 points on the held-out test cohort in our oncology tasks. To overcome these challenges, we shift focus to improving the improver and propose Semantic PID-RSI , an evidence-guided control-theoretic framework that recursively evolves CliAgent. Inspired by classic Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) control in dynamical systems [ 3 ] , our improver replaces unconstrained reflection with three parallel, complementary diagnostic analyses over a shared batch of candidate modification plans. Specifically, Proportional (P) current-error analysis isolates concrete clinical failure modes observed in the active agent and grounds each proposal in a specific performance gap; Integral (I) historical-evidence analysis synthesizes longitudinal records from both successful and unsuccessful prior trials, preventing repetitive dead-ends while identifying when changed conditions justify revisiting an earlier hypothesis; and Derivative (D) response-and-risk analysis estimates intervention scope, dependencies, and regression risks prior to execution (D-pre), while reconciling expected versus realized behavioral shifts after evaluation (D-post). Evaluating a common candidate batch through these three lenses makes immediate error correction, long-term trial memory, and regression risks directly comparable before allocating evaluation budget. Furthermore, Semantic PID-RSI enforces disciplined workflow evolution through behavior contracts and independent adoption. Rather than applying free-form edits to CliAgent, each selected plan is compiled into a formal behavior contract [ 17 ] specifying the intended behavioral delta, invariant behaviors to preserve, and explicit falsification criteria. Revised agents undergo staged probing followed by full development-set evaluation, after which an independent adoption judge evaluates the measured response against the contract to decide whether to update the incumbent program. Both adopted and rejected trials, together with their D-post discrepancy analyses, are archived into a structured research history, closing the loop between hypothesis formulation and empirical evidence. Instantiated and validated on three challenging oncology tasks (namely cancer pT staging, pN staging, and survival prognosis), evolving CliAgent with Semantic PID-RSI on a lightweight 3B-activated open-weight model achieves substantial held-out test gains (up to +6.27 C-index points over the initial agent).
