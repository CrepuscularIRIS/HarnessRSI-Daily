arXiv: 2611.31475    title: SpatialCraft: Evolving Procedural Intelligence from Experience and Skills for Spatial Reasoning Agents

[abstract]
Spatial reasoning agents increasingly rely on external tools, such as 3D reconstruction, segmentation, and geometric computation, to answer questions that a multimodal large language model (MLLM) cannot resolve from pixels alone. Tool access, however, shifts the bottleneck to orchestration: the agent must decide which tools to call, in what order, and how to interpret their outputs, yet a stateless agent repeats the same mistakes across tasks. We ask whether a frozen tool-using agent can improve its orchestration from its own interaction history, without parameter updates. We present SpatialCraft, a self-evolving framework that maintains two forms of external knowledge: Experiences, condition-action tactics distilled by contrasting rollouts of the same task, and Skills, multi-step procedures with explicit activation and termination conditions. Three designs couple the two streams. Experiences can be anchored to a Skill, appearing only while it is active rather than once per task. A Contrastive Utility Score estimates each Experience's effect by randomly withholding it across rollouts of the same task, separating its contribution from task difficulty, and Experiences with consistently positive utility are consolidated into the Skills they support. Across five spatial benchmarks and four backbones, SpatialCraft achieves the best accuracy in all 20 settings, showing that a frozen agent can learn to orchestrate spatial tools from its own experience.

[intro/head from cached fulltext]
Spatial reasoning, the ability to infer 3D distances, orientations, and object relations from visual observations, is a fundamental prerequisite for embodied agents and robotic manipulation [ 67 , 45 , 42 , 31 , 18 ] . Yet multimodal large language models (MLLMs) frequently fail at complex spatial queries when relying solely on internal parametric knowledge [ 10 , 13 , 8 , 42 , 31 , 18 , 35 , 54 , 60 ] . Consequently, a growing line of work equips MLLMs with external spatial tools, such as 3D reconstruction, metric depth estimation, and pose estimation, enabling them to explicitly compute metric geometric quantities rather than relying on ungrounded visual perception [ 16 , 11 , 15 , 45 , 41 , 80 , 40 ] . However, tool integration shifts the bottleneck from perception to orchestration. A tool-augmented agent must decide which tools to call, in what order, and how to interpret their outputs. Because these decisions are interdependent, a single tactical error can derail the entire reasoning chain, yet most spatial agents operate statelessly, discarding trial-and-error insights after each episode. Supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning can internalize spatial and tool-use competence [ 10 , 8 , 50 , 36 , 38 , 77 , 11 , 80 ] , but must be repeated whenever the backbone or toolset changes. An alternative is parameter-update-free self-evolution, in which a frozen agent accumulates knowledge in an external memory [ 53 , 76 , 63 , 74 , 73 ] . SMA [ 72 ] shows that such memory benefits tool-free spatial reasoning. For tool-augmented agents, however, useful knowledge spans both multi-step tool procedures and step-level tactics, raising the challenge of how to structure, validate, and integrate it without accumulating spurious or redundant heuristics. To address this challenge, we draw on the hierarchical nature of tool use, which prior work captures with two complementary forms of reusable knowledge [ 32 ] : procedural Skills , which orchestrate multi-step tool pipelines (e.g., reconstructing a scene before querying distances) [ 57 , 63 , 78 , 46 ] , and tactical Experiences , which guide localized decisions (e.g., re-prompting a segmentation model upon detecting a failure) [ 76 , 55 , 47 ] . In spatial reasoning, the two are intertwined, as a tactic’s usefulness depends on the procedural stage: re-prompting segmentation helps during object grounding but is irrelevant during metric comparison. This suggests structuring knowledge hierarchically, with tactical Experiences anchored to the procedural Skills they support. Tactics that prove consistently effective can then be absorbed into the procedure itself, so that the Skill evolves and the Experience bank stays compact, much as human expertise compiles practiced rules into procedures [ 1 ] . Beyond structural organization, a self-evolving agent requires empirical validation of its accumulated knowledge. MLLMs often distill lessons that are linguistically plausible but practically misleading [ 29 ] , and retaining such untested heuristics lets the memory accumulate errors alongside competence. Correlating a lesson with successful episodes, as in reward-based memory valuation [ 74 , 72 ] , is insufficient because the correlation is confounded by task difficulty: heuristics retrieved for inherently easier tasks may appear effective regardless of their actual impact. Isolating the utility of a lesson instead requires a controlled comparison. By randomly showing an Experience in only a subset of the parallel rollouts of the same task [ 52 ] , the agent can contrast their outcomes to isolate the effect of the tactic from task difficulty, at no extra interaction cost, since these rollouts are already collected to distill Experiences. Building on these insights, we present SpatialCraft , a parameter-update-free framework that co-evolves tactical Experiences and procedural Skills for tool-augmented spatial agents. During exploration, SpatialCraft leverages multi-trajectory rollouts to distill Experiences, anchoring each strictly to the active Skill. The framework evaluates each Experience using a Contrastive Utility Score, which estimates its contribution by comparing within-task rollouts with and without the tactic. Experiences that systematically improve outcomes are consolidated into their anchor Skills, while ineffective ones are retired. Skills themselves are refined through trajectory feedback, with proposed revisions rigorously verified against prior decision states before adoption. During deployment, the accumulated dual-level knowledge base is frozen, enabling the agent to seamlessly retrieve task-level insights and trigger context-aware tactics precisely when the corresponding Skill is invoked, so tactics surface where they apply.
