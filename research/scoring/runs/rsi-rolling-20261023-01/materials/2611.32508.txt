arXiv: 2611.32508    title: RecurSciBench: Benchmarking Recursive Self-Improvement in Data-Driven Scientific Research

[abstract]
Large language model (LLM)-based agents are advancing the automation of data-driven scientific research. Recent advances in recursive self-improvement (RSI) offer the potential for these agents to continuously improve through experience, but evaluating transfer across scientific tasks requires executable, scientifically verifiable tasks and independent assessment of self-improvement gains. We introduce RecurSciBench, a scalable benchmark for evaluating RSI in data-driven scientific research. Its RecurSciPipe framework automatically constructs scientific modeling and data analysis tasks from raw datasets, with execution-based verification and scientific validity checks. RecurSciBench spans four scientific domains, multiple data modalities, and varied scientific objectives. We evaluate four RSI systems by comparing their initial and evolved states on independent tasks after evolution on source tasks, covering within-type, cross-type, and cross-domain transfer. Experiments show that the benefits of self-improvement vary across agent systems and scientific domains. Positive overall gains can coexist with regressions on individual tasks. Experience can transfer across task types and benefit other domains even without measurable gains in the source domain.

Data-driven approaches play a central role in scientific research, enabling researchers to uncover patterns and relationships through scientific data analysis and quantitative modeling [ 8 , 21 ] . Recent advances in large language model (LLM)-based agents are increasingly automating these research workflows, from data interpretation and method selection to implementation and validation [ 2 , 6 ] . Beyond automating individual research tasks, recursive self-improvement (RSI) enables agents to accumulate experience and iteratively update their own problem-solving mechanisms, including persistent memory, execution harnesses, and improvement strategies [ 13 , 27 , 7 ] . In data-driven scientific research, such updates offer the potential to transfer knowledge and strategies acquired from previous investigations to new analysis and modeling tasks. However, whether such persistent updates translate into generalizable performance gains on independent scientific tasks remains unclear. Systematically evaluating recursive self-improvement in data-driven scientific research requires addressing three key challenges. (1) Coverage and extensibility across scientific settings. Benchmarks for LLMs and agents have progressed from knowledge-based questions to scientific research tasks [ 18 , 2 , 22 ] . However, scientific research spans diverse domains, data modalities, and modeling assumptions, while many existing benchmarks draw on expert-curated or paper-derived tasks, limiting their extensibility to new scientific datasets and research objectives. (2) Ensuring scientific validity beyond executability. Executable tasks and automated scoring alone do not guarantee reliable scientific evaluation. Inappropriate modeling assumptions, inconsistent data processing, or unsuitable evaluation criteria can produce misleading results, requiring checks of both execution correctness and scientific validity. (3) Evaluating experience transfer across research granularities. Although existing RSI benchmarks have explored iterative improvement and cross-task experience reuse in general-purpose agent settings [ 29 , 11 , 5 ] , the transfer of experience across different granularities of scientific research remains underexplored. In practice, scientific investigations often involve activities of varying scope, making it challenging to assess whether experience acquired from more focused tasks can improve performance on more comprehensive research problems. To address these challenges, we introduce RecurSciBench , a benchmark for evaluating recursive self-improvement in data-driven scientific research. We develop RecurSciPipe , a four-agent framework that automatically constructs executable scientific data analysis and modeling tasks from raw scientific datasets. Starting with scientific data profiling, RecurSciPipe jointly designs and builds related analysis and full modeling tasks that share scientific context while retaining distinct objectives and evaluation criteria. The generated tasks undergo execution checks and scientific validity assessments to verify their executability, information isolation, and consistency with scientific requirements. The resulting benchmark spans four scientific domains and multiple data modalities, providing different sources of scientific experience for agent evolution and independent tasks for evaluating its transfer. Using RecurSciBench , we evaluate four RSI systems while preserving their native self-improvement and task-solving workflows under fixed backbone model weights. Each system evolves on designated source tasks, after which its frozen evolved state is compared against the corresponding initial state on the same independent tasks (Figure 1 ). We consider three distinct transfer settings: within-type, cross-type, and cross-domain transfer, examining how self-improvement outcomes vary across experience sources and scientific domains. We find that evolution outcomes differ across agent systems and scientific domains, with half of the evolved states showing negative overall gains. The same evolved state can improve performance in one domain while reducing it in another. Positive overall gains can coexist with regressions on a majority of test tasks. Data analysis experience may improve modeling even when evolution on modeling tasks yields a negative gain. Experience from one domain can also benefit tasks in other domains even without measurable gains in the same domain. Our key contributions are as follows: We introduce RecurSciBench , an extensible RSI benchmark for data-driven scientific research, covering four scientific domains and multiple data modalities. Disjoint evolution and test pools enable frozen-state evaluation of self-improvement transfer. We develop RecurSciPipe to automatically construct linked scientific analysis and modeling task families with data, scorers, and task descriptions, verified for executability and scientific validity.
